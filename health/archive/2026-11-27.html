Medicine and Research

Engineered molecular glue aims to stabilize aging eggs

STAT

Researchers are using engineered molecular glue to stabilize cohesin, a protein complex that holds chromosomes together. As this glue degrades with age, it causes genetic errors in eggs. The intervention aims to address the leading cause of age-related infertility and miscarriage in women over 40.

Medical Xpress

A new platform reveals how specific cancer-causing mutations rewire cell metabolism to fuel tumor growth. The study highlights that tumors possess distinct metabolic needs even when located within the same organ, offering potential new targets for treatment. Cancer cells remain hungry, regardless of the specific menu.

Heart cells may use non-direct signaling

Medical Xpress

New evidence suggests heart cells transmit electrical signals through methods beyond direct connections. The heart beats at least 3 billion times over a human lifetime, maintaining its rhythm while the body sleeps, works, or exercises. The organ continues to function without requiring much thought from its owner.

Brain immune cells linked to motor memory loss

Medical Xpress

Learning new movements changes the myelin insulating nerve fibers in the brain. A new study suggests that immune cells may weaken these motor memories by removing that insulation. The brain appears to be actively editing its own physical connections as it learns new skills.

Medical Xpress

Colorectal tumors exhibit high levels of heterogeneity, meaning cancer cells within a single patient display divergent behaviors. This diversity complicates treatment, as different cell states respond differently to therapy. Tumors are not uniform, which continues to reduce the overall efficacy of clinical interventions.

Mindfulness linked to healthier social media use

The Hindu

A review of 43 studies involving 28,000 participants suggests that mindfulness is associated with healthier social media habits and lower levels of depression, anxiety, and loneliness. Mindfulness is defined as observing thoughts without judgement. It appears that paying attention to one's own mind helps with the feed.

Read more at The Hindu →

Study finds weak links between creativity and mental health

wisevoter.com

A study of 14,600 twins published in Molecular Psychiatry found weak associations between artistic creativity and diagnoses including schizophrenia and anxiety disorders. The research analyzed data from the Swedish Twin Registry. The long-standing question of genius and madness remains largely a matter of statistical nuance.

Read more at wisevoter.com →

Psilocybin users continue standard mental health care

psypost.org

A study published in Psychological Medicine found that people with depression who use psilocybin do not abandon conventional psychiatric services. They use telehealth and prescription medications at rates similar to non-users, while reporting higher levels of unmet needs. The psychedelic experience does not replace the appointment book.

Read more at psypost.org →

Creativity and mental health links remain complex

idw-online.de

Researchers from the Max Planck Institute and Karolinska Institute found that while high levels of artistic creativity are associated with mental health problems, this correlation is largely absent for scientific creativity. The study in Molecular Psychiatry suggests the relationship between genius and madness is more complex than expected.

Research from King's College London involving 9,632 adolescents found that higher caffeine intake is linked to smaller brain surface area and increased mental health problems six months later. Peer-reviewed clinical and public-health findings that landed recently. Preprints say so on the card. No miracle claims.

Common supplements rival antibiotics in treating severe gum disease

ScienceDaily · 2026-11-26

A combination of omega-3 and low-dose aspirin produced results comparable to antibiotics when used alongside standard treatment for severe gum disease, with benefits persisting long after supplementation ended. The surprising discovery could offer an alternative for some patients while reducing reliance on antibiotics.

Integrated wastewater and clinical genomic surveillance for high-resolution tracking of measles virus evolution and transmission

Nature Medicine · 2026-11-26

Nature Medicine, Published online: 26 November 2026, doi:10.1038/s41591-026-04754-3 Whole-genome sequencing and phylogenetic analyses of wastewater and surveillance samples during the 2024−2025 measles outbreak in South Africa reveal previously undetected inter-provincial spread and transmission links, suggesting that wastewater-integrated whole-genome surveillance can improve measles monitoring and control efforts.

World’s largest trial of robotic knee replacement reveals surprising results

ScienceDaily · 2026-11-26

Robot-assisted knee replacement surgery offers greater surgical precision, but a landmark clinical trial has revealed a surprising limitation. Despite the advanced technology, patients experienced no meaningful improvements in pain, mobility, or recovery compared with conventional surgery after one year.

Scientists discover a surprising twist in how the body burns calories

ScienceDaily · 2026-11-25

Researchers have discovered that a drug can significantly increase the body's energy expenditure without clearly activating calorie-burning brown fat. The unexpected finding reveals a new challenge in developing obesity treatments that harness the body's natural fat-burning machinery.
