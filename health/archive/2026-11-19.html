White blood cell DNA may strengthen cancer signals

health.economictimes.indiatimes.com

A four-year study of 1,480 participants across 43 Indian hospitals found that higher levels of circulating DNA in cancer patients improved detection sensitivity. The research, published in PNAS, challenges the long-held assumption that DNA released by white blood cells dilutes cancer signals in blood-based tests.

The Hindu

Seven children with Allan-Herndon-Dudley syndrome will receive a year of tiratricol for free on November 30. The initiative, led by paediatrician Dr. Sivaranjani Santosh and Access to Medicines India, reduced the annual treatment cost from 9 crore rupees to 2.5 lakh rupees per patient.

Peer-reviewed clinical and public-health findings that landed recently. Preprints say so on the card. No miracle claims.

A little-known protein could be a powerful weapon against Alzheimer’s

ScienceDaily · 2026-11-18

Scientists have uncovered a powerful natural defense against Alzheimer’s disease that could open the door to new treatments. By boosting a protein called SORLA, researchers significantly reduced toxic tau tangles, protected connections between brain cells, and prevented some of the brain shrinkage associated with neurodegeneration in mice. Removing.

A rare disease reveals a hidden flaw in the immune system

ScienceDaily · 2026-11-18

Scientists have discovered that a rare, potentially life-threatening inflammatory disorder is driven by malfunctioning immune cells, overturning three decades of scientific understanding. The breakthrough points to a promising new treatment using existing medications, with one severely affected infant already showing substantial improvement.

MIT discovers that aging weakens the brain’s nighttime cleaning system

ScienceDaily · 2026-11-18

MIT researchers have discovered that the brain’s natural waste-removal system becomes significantly less effective during sleep as people age. The decline is linked to weaker deep-sleep brain waves and changes in blood flow, potentially offering new clues about why aging increases the risk of Alzheimer's disease and dementia.

Tiny magnets deep inside the brain could transform Parkinson’s treatment

ScienceDaily · 2026-11-18

Scientists have developed a new technique that uses magnetic nanoparticles to stimulate deep brain regions, dramatically improving Parkinson’s-like movement problems in mice without permanently implanted electrodes. The breakthrough could pave the way for less invasive treatments using injectable particles and wearable magnetic headbands.
