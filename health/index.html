Genomic testing often misses cancer mutation targets

cambridgebrc.nihr.ac.uk

Researchers at the NIHR Biomedical Research Centre in Cambridge found that half of targetable cancer mutations are located in the wrong cancer type. Current testing is often organized by where a tumor originates rather than its DNA. Substantial opportunities for targeted treatments are being overlooked annually.

New index identifies tumor origins

Medical Xpress

The Clonal Relatedness Index can determine if tumors in the same patient share a common origin. Published in The Journal of Molecular Diagnostics, the findings aim to assist in managing complex digestive and abdominal cancers. It is a new way to track where a tumor actually began.

Sibeprenlimab preserves kidney function in IgA nephropathy

uab.edu

A UAB study found that sibeprenlimab preserved kidney function over two years in patients with IgA nephropathy. The drug blocks a protein involved in producing abnormal antibodies. Only one patient receiving the treatment experienced a major kidney event, compared with 28 patients in the placebo group.
