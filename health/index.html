Medicine and Research

Merck and Moderna's mRNA vaccine cuts melanoma recurrence

NPR

Merck and Moderna released trial results showing their personalised mRNA vaccine, given with an existing immunotherapy, significantly reduced cancer recurrence and death in patients with melanoma. NPR says it is the biggest test yet of the mRNA cancer vaccine concept.

Read more at NPR →

Pfizer's Lyme disease vaccine shows 70% efficacy

medicaleconomics.com

Pfizer and Valneva say their experimental Lyme disease vaccine cut tick-borne infections by more than 70% in a late-stage trial of 9,400 people aged 5 and up. The trial narrowly missed its statistical threshold, but the companies plan to submit the data to regulators. Statistics and ticks both have thresholds.

Read more at medicaleconomics.com →

Precision T-cell therapy stalls treatment-resistant cancers

Medical Xpress

Early results from the first human trial of the drug invikafusp alfa suggest it may be able to manage cancers that have resisted most other treatments, including standard immunotherapy, Medical Xpress reports on the clinical trial findings.

Read more at Medical Xpress →

Skipping blood pressure pills wipes out heart protection

republicworld.com

An analysis of 91,339 patients across nine trials found that consistent blood pressure medication use cut major cardiovascular events by 11%. Patients taking their pills less than 80% of the time showed little to no protection against heart attack and stroke. The pills, it turns out, work when taken.

Read more at republicworld.com →

Petrelintide may cause fewer stomach side effects than GLP-1 drugs

medicalnewstoday.com

In a phase 2 trial, people taking the experimental amylin drug petrelintide lost up to 9.8% of body weight over 28 weeks, with gastrointestinal side effects similar to placebo. It is not yet available and needs more trials, Medical News Today reports. Patience remains in trial.

Read more at medicalnewstoday.com →

Non-surgical knee procedure gives lasting arthritis pain relief

foxnews.com

A procedure that temporarily blocks extra blood vessels in the knee gave 80% of 194 patients with knee osteoarthritis significant pain relief lasting at least a year. It could offer an alternative for people hoping to avoid total knee replacement, Fox News reports.

Read more at foxnews.com →

Study maps new treatment targets for common bowel cancer

Medical Xpress

An international team led by RCSI University of Medicine and Health Sciences and The Institute of Cancer Research, London, has identified potential new ways to treat the most common type of colorectal cancer. The study provides a roadmap for future treatment research.

Read more at Medical Xpress →

Clot-buster benefit before thrombectomy depends on stroke cause

neurologylive.com

A secondary analysis of the BRIDGE-TNK trial found that giving tenecteplase before thrombectomy improved 90-day outcomes in patients with cardioembolic stroke but not in those with large artery atherosclerosis. Rates of serious bleeding were similar in both groups, NeurologyLive reports.

Read more at neurologylive.com →

Tegoprubart improves quality of life after kidney transplant

hcplive.com

In the phase 2 BESTOW trial, the investigational antibody tegoprubart was linked to greater gains in quality of life and fewer medication-related symptoms than the standard drug tacrolimus at one year in first-time kidney transplant patients, HCPLive reports.

Read more at hcplive.com →

Good diet may offset genetic risk of high cholesterol in Indians

The Indian Express

A study of 1,020 adults in Chennai suggests that among Indians genetically prone to high LDL cholesterol, those eating a medium or high quality diet could completely offset the genetic risk. Diets heavy in polished rice and oil worked against healthy levels. Grandmother's genes meet grandmother's cooking.

Read more at The Indian Express →

Both low and high testosterone linked to irregular heartbeat

sciencealert.com

A review in the Journal of the Endocrine Society led by the University of Kansas Medical Center found that both low and high testosterone are linked to higher risk of a heart rhythm disorder. Risk may be lowest in the middle of the normal range. Moderation wins again.

Read more at sciencealert.com →

Centre bans reuse of single-use medical devices

VOH Network

The Union Health Ministry has banned reuse of single-use medical and surgical devices across healthcare organisations, after reports that disposable devices were being sterilised and reused in some private and corporate hospitals, including in cardiology, nephrology and oncology. Single use now means once.

India targets mercury-free healthcare by 2030

Nation Path India

India aims to phase out mercury thermometers and blood pressure monitors from healthcare by 2030. Fourteen states have completed assessments and 12 have issued phase-out orders, with support from the health and environment ministries, WHO and UNEP. The old thermometer in the drawer has been put on notice.
