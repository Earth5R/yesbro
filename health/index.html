Medicine and Research

Moderna's personalised mRNA melanoma vaccine succeeds in a phase 3 trial

STAT

Moderna and Merck presented the first phase 3 study showing a customised mRNA therapy, intismeran, could improve outcomes for patients with melanoma, at ESMO in Madrid. 'Most people in the field thought this was never going to work,' CEO Stephane Bancel told STAT, after 10 years of work.

Read more at STAT →

Amgen's Imdelltra with immunotherapy extends survival in small cell lung cancer

The Hindu

Amgen's Imdelltra combined with AstraZeneca's Imfinzi reduced the risk of death by 58% compared with Imfinzi alone in extensive-stage small cell lung cancer. Patients given both lived a median of 21.1 months against 12.7 months, according to data presented at a major medical meeting.

Read more at The Hindu →

Next-generation KRAS G12C drug extends survival in previously treated lung cancer

biospace.com

A global phase 3 trial found an investigational targeted therapy extended overall survival compared with approved treatments in previously treated KRAS G12C-mutated non-small cell lung cancer. The results were presented at ESMO 2026 and published in The New England Journal of Medicine, Tennessee Oncology said.

Read more at biospace.com →

Merck's targeted chemotherapy cuts risk of death by a third in advanced endometrial cancer

STAT

Sacituzumab tirumotecan, developed by Merck and China's Kelun-Biotech, cut the risk of death by one-third in a phase 3 study in advanced endometrial cancer, STAT reports. Merck is running 18 global phase 3 studies of the drug. The full piece is for STAT+ subscribers.

Read more at STAT →

Personalised vaccine plus radiotherapy extends progression-free survival in advanced tumours

oncodaily.com

In the randomised phase 2 iNATURE trial presented at ESMO 2026, a personalised neoantigen peptide vaccine combined with targeted radiotherapy raised median progression-free survival from 3.1 to 10.3 months in patients with heavily pretreated advanced solid tumours, compared with placebo plus radiotherapy.

Read more at oncodaily.com →
More from Medicine and Research

Triple therapy shrinks tumours in hard-to-treat head and neck cancers, trial shows

nz.news.yahoo.com

An international trial involving the Institute of Cancer Research, London, and the Royal Marsden found a new triple therapy may shrink tumours in head and neck cancers not linked to high-risk HPV that had spread or returned. Researchers said it could 'transform outcomes' for these patients.

Read more at nz.news.yahoo.com →

A liquid painted on teeth can stop cavities in young children, major trial confirms

ScienceDaily

A major University of Michigan clinical trial found silver diamine fluoride, an inexpensive liquid applied in seconds, can stop tooth decay in young children without drilling, injections or anaesthesia. Researchers say it could spare many children painful procedures and pave the way for broader FDA-approved use.

Read more at ScienceDaily →

CAR T-cell therapy zola-cel shows early promise in multiple sclerosis

consultqd.clevelandclinic.org

Preliminary results from the phase 1 Breakfree-2 trial found a single infusion of zola-cel, a CD19-directed CAR T-cell therapy, led to stable and even improved clinical indicators at six months in highly active relapsing and progressive multiple sclerosis, with mostly good safety, Cleveland Clinic reports.

Read more at consultqd.clevelandclinic.org →

Review separates established GLP-1 drug risks from uncertain safety signals

news-medical.net

A review in the Journal of Internal Medicine sorts the safety evidence on GLP-1 receptor agonists, from pancreatitis to thyroid cancer and vision loss, into established risks and uncertain signals. It says a patient's individual risk, expected benefit and alternatives matter more than alarming headlines.

Read more at news-medical.net →

Medical societies set out four life stages for women's heart health

STAT

The American Heart Association, the American Academy of Pediatrics and two obstetrics societies issued a framework to unite clinicians and patients across a woman's life. It aims to tackle cardiovascular disease as the leading cause of death in women, STAT reports, amid wide gaps in care.

Read more at STAT →

Memantine modestly improved 'lupus brain fog' in a small trial

Medical Dialogues

A small randomised, placebo-controlled phase 2 trial in the Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases found memantine modestly improved cognitive function in people with lupus and neuropsychiatric symptoms after 12 weeks. The authors say larger trials are needed to confirm whether it works.

Read more at Medical Dialogues →

Intravenous arginine did not shorten sickle cell pain crises, trial finds

Medical Dialogues

A randomised trial in JAMA found intravenous arginine did not significantly reduce the time to resolve acute pain episodes in children and young adults with sickle cell disease compared with placebo. The study was stopped early because the treatment was unlikely to show the expected benefit.

Read more at Medical Dialogues →

Adding pembrolizumab to radiotherapy brought no benefit in early endometrial cancer

oncodaily.com

The phase 3 NRG-GY020 trial presented at ESMO 2026 found adding pembrolizumab to adjuvant radiotherapy did not significantly improve recurrence-free survival in early-stage, mismatch repair-deficient endometrial cancer. The combination was also associated with more grade 3 to 4 toxicity.

Read more at oncodaily.com →

Most people carry a 'high genetic risk' for at least three common conditions, study finds

The Hindu

University of Exeter researchers described the 'average' human genome and found most people could be classed as high genetic risk for three of 28 common conditions, including diabetes and cardiovascular disease, even if they never develop them. The study appears in Genome Medicine.

Read more at The Hindu →

Public Health India

Bengaluru bus drivers, traffic police and sanitation workers show reduced lung function

The Hindu

A three-year study of over 10,000 frontline workers in Bengaluru found higher-than-normal early lung-function abnormalities in all groups screened. Pourakarmikas had the highest share, with around 45% showing signs of reduced lung function, followed by BMTC drivers and traffic police, The Hindu reports.

Read more at The Hindu →

West Bengal's caesarean rate nearly doubles to almost one in two births

The Hindu

NFHS-6 data for 2023-24 puts caesarean births in West Bengal at 44.5%, up from 23.8% in 2015-16, The Hindu reports. In 2015 the state health department had suggested 'prescription audits' of caesareans in private and government facilities. The memo, at least, was delivered on time.

Read more at The Hindu →

Children with glaucoma often reach care too late, AIIMS experts warn

Outlook India

In an editorial in the Indian Journal of Ophthalmology, AIIMS doctors Viney Gupta and Arnav Panigrahi say many Indian children with glaucoma reach specialist care only after the disease has advanced. They call for treating childhood glaucoma as a public health concern, with earlier diagnosis and faster referrals.

Read more at Outlook India →

IIT Bombay develops a low-cost device to screen for anaemia and sickle cell disease

The Hindu

IIT Bombay researchers led by Mahesh Tirumkudulu built a low-cost device that screens for anaemia and sickle cell disease using a drop of blood. It measures how far a blood smear spreads across a glass slide, since blood from different people gives different smear lengths.

Read more at The Hindu →

In an article in Oncology Reviews, AIIMS Delhi surgeons describe 'VIP syndrome', where high-profile patients get preferential treatment that departs from standard protocols. They say it can mean unnecessary tests, prolonged ICU stays and compromised safety. The VIPs, presumably, were the last to hear.

An independent expert report seen by AFP warns diphtheria, whooping cough and polio outbreaks could follow Bangladesh's measles epidemic, which has killed more than 1,200 children and infected 220,000 people since March. The report calls it a warning sign of a deteriorating immunisation system.

The US has recorded more than 4,580 measles cases in 2026 as of 22 October, more than double the total for 2025, according to CDC data reported by NPR. The vaccination status of 96% of cases was unvaccinated or unknown, and Pennsylvania has confirmed at least 1,338 cases.
