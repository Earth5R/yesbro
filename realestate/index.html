This new building material pulls carbon out of the air

ScienceDaily · 2026-01-21

Worcester Polytechnic Institute engineers describe an enzyme-driven building material that cures in hours and can sequester more than 6 kilograms of CO2 per cubic metre in reported tests, versus hundreds of kilograms emitted making ordinary concrete. Recyclable panels are part of the pitch.

Cardboard-confined rammed earth cuts cement from low-rise walls

ScienceDaily · 2025-09-22

RMIT University researchers present cardboard-confined rammed earth made from cardboard, soil and water, claiming about one quarter of concrete's carbon footprint and under one third of the cost for suitable low-rise walls. Hot climates get the thermal-mass benefit.

ScienceDaily · 2025-02-19

Newcastle University researchers replaced sand in mortar with silica aerogel and recycled PET and report heat-loss cuts of up to 55% versus conventional mortar while meeting masonry strength standards. Thermal bridges between bricks are the target.
