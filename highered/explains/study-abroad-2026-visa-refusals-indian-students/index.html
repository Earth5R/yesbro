Study abroad in 2026: visa refusals are up, arrivals are down, and the smart money is asking about ROI

The US, Canada, the UK and Australia all still say they welcome international students. Their numbers say something more complicated. Here is what Indian students and parents should take from this week's data.

By Yes Bro Desk · 25 October 2026 · 3 min read

For a generation of Indian families, the study abroad plan had three steps. Get the admit, get the loan, get on the plane. In 2026 the middle of that plan has a new step, which is wondering whether the visa will come at all. This week's numbers explain why.

The United States: fewer arrivals, a costly proposal

Indian student arrivals in the US on F1 visas fell 39% this August compared with a year earlier, the lowest August level since 2020, Gulte reported, pointing to stricter immigration policy, heavier document checks and scarce visa slots.

The bigger long term worry is work after graduation. The Trump administration has proposed charging colleges as much as $100,000 for their international students to take part in OPT, the post study work programme, Inside Higher Ed reported. It is a proposal, not a final rule. But for many Indian students OPT is the reason the US maths works, so watch it closely.

Canada: a busy August inside a much smaller year

Canada welcomed 37,090 new study permit holders in August, nearly six times earlier monthly figures, The Indian Express reported using IRCC data. Do not let the spike fool you. New student arrivals from January to August were 71% lower than in the same period of 2024. August is when autumn students arrive, so a monthly jump says little about the trend.

Australia: the welcome mat and the refusal letter

Australia has kept its planning level for new overseas student commencements at 295,000 for 2026 and confirmed the same for 2027, The Australia Today reported. Yet the overall student visa refusal rate reached 24.2% in June 2026, up from 7.9% a decade earlier, and families in Nepal, Bangladesh and India say the decisions send a different message. The standard student visa charge for a main applicant is AUD 2,500, Tuko reported, with a reduced charge for citizens of 23 countries across the Pacific, Timor-Leste and ASEAN. India is not on that list.

An ANU paper by Alan Gamlen, Peter McDonald and Sahar Zia argues that student numbers are always a migration question as well as an education one, Future Campus reported. Translation for families: policy can swing during your degree, so plan for it.

The UK: tighter on who gets in and who stays

BusinessDay reports the UK is cutting student visas, especially for applicants from Nigeria and Pakistan, and that the ability to pay fees no longer guarantees entry. The same report says staying on to look for work after graduation has become harder. The report focuses on Nigerian and Pakistani applicants, but the direction of travel matters for everyone.

Asking the ROI question properly

None of this means studying abroad is a bad idea. It means the old automatic assumption is gone. Ashoka University co-founder Pranav Gupta says rising costs, visa rules and new options are making Indian families more selective about the real return of a foreign degree, and that students should look beyond the location or foreign name of an institution.

Some of that global education is also coming to India in a new form. University World News reports attention is shifting from foreign universities opening full campuses to setting up research hubs, such as the University of Manchester's planned centre in Bengaluru.

A practical checklist

Price the full degree including living costs, then price what happens if post study work rights change. Apply to more than one country. Keep a credible plan B in India. And build an application that stands out on substance, because when refusal rates rise, a strong, verifiable profile matters more.

For school students, one way to build that is original research. Visas are outside your control. Your profile is not.
