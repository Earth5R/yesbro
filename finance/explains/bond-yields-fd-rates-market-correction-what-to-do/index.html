Bond yields at a three and a half year high, FD rates creeping up: what it means for your money

Stocks are down, gold is well off its peak and safe returns are quietly getting better. A plain guide to what rising yields mean for fixed deposits, gilt funds and your SIPs.

By Yes Bro Desk · 25 October 2026 · 3 min read

This was a week when the boring parts of finance got interesting. The stock market fell for four straight sessions, but the real story for savers was in bonds and bank deposits, where returns have been quietly rising. Here is what happened, and how to think about it. This is a general explainer, not advice for your specific situation.

The week in one paragraph

The Sensex fell 1,605.44 points, or 2.15%, and the Nifty 546.15 points, or 2.36%, in a holiday shortened week, Business Standard reported. The Nifty Bank index dropped 2.79% to 54,287.35, Mint reported. Foreign portfolio investors have pulled ₹48,056 crore from Indian equities so far in October, after ₹35,861 crore in September, though they turned net buyers on two days last week, Business Standard reported. The common thread is oil, with Brent nearing $120 according to BusinessLine.

The bond market is doing the talking

The 10 year government bond yield rose to 7.474% on 23 October from 7.193% on 6 October, about 28 basis points since the RBI's October policy review, Business Standard reported. A year ago it was about 6.54%, and the level is the highest in three and a half years, according to Mint. Mint points to rising US yields, with the US 10 year Treasury near 5%, higher crude prices and tighter monetary policy at home.

Why should you care? Bond prices move opposite to yields. If you already own a gilt fund, rising yields have hurt its value. If you are about to invest, you lock in a higher yield. Mint reports fund managers are split on whether to buy now or wait for a better entry point. Nobody rings a bell at the top.

Your bank is paying a little more

Deposit rates are following. ICICI Bank raised FD rates by 10 basis points on select tenures and Indian Bank by 15 basis points on its 555 day and 777 day special FDs, with senior citizens at ICICI now earning up to 7.20%, The Economic Times reported. Some small finance banks go higher. Suryoday Small Finance Bank offers senior citizens 8.5% on five year deposits, and Jana Small Finance Bank 8%, ET reported.

One likely reason banks are competing for money is that, in the latest fortnight, deposit growth slowed to 16.7% and credit growth to 18.2%, with a credit deposit ratio of 81.59%, ET reported. When loans grow faster than deposits, banks pay more to attract savers. Higher rates at smaller banks come with more risk, so check deposit insurance limits before you chase the top number.

Gold has cooled

MCX gold futures settled at ₹1,42,024 per 10 grams on Friday, 21.43% below the record ₹1,80,779 set on 29 January, and down 4.7% in October, Mint reported. If you bought at the peak, that hurts. If you have been waiting, the Diwali price is lower than it was in January.

Stay invested or trim?

On equity funds, Mint highlights an Abakkus Mutual Fund analysis of Nifty 50 Total Return Index data from April 2005 to September 2026. At the end of September the Nifty stood at 22,716, below its 2 January peak of 26,329 and needing a 15.9% gain to get back there. The point of studying two decades of data is perspective. Corrections are part of owning stocks, and selling in a panic locks in the loss.

A simple framework

Keep an emergency fund in safe deposits, where higher FD rates are now a small gift. Match money you need within a few years to fixed income. Keep long term SIPs running if your goals have not changed. And if you want your savings to reflect your values, learn how ESG claims are checked.
