A catalyst that improves with use turns polystyrene into benzoic acid

Bioengineer.org

A team led by Xuefeng Jiang at East China Normal University, with Hainan University's Yujie Song, converted polystyrene into high-purity benzoic acid at room temperature and ambient pressure under visible light. azom.com

Researchers writing in Communications Sustainability tested what happens when cellulose contamination stays in recycled PET, processing it under tightly controlled conditions into contamination-tolerant composites with cellulose-rich waste. The idea is to skip removing every trace of paper. Recycling's oldest chore may get shorter.

A method to unmake pDCPD composites and keep the fibres

Bioengineer.org

Researchers at the University of Texas at Austin and Sandia National Laboratories dissolve pDCPD composites using an environmentally friendly solvent and a ruthenium-based catalyst, leaving a powder reusable in new plastics. Carbon and glass reinforcing fibres come out in pristine form, avoiding the waste of incineration.

Enzyme-laced polyester films that break down in the deep sea

intelligentliving.co

A University of Tokyo team with JAMSTEC and Gunma University mixed trace natural enzymes into compostable polyester films during processing. After five months at 757 metres, the enzyme films were almost gone while identical films without enzymes lost at most 3.4% of their weight, a Nature paper reports.
