Plastic Packaging

ExxonMobil backed study finds PE packs often beat the alternatives

packaginginsights.com

A lifecycle study of 47 packaging systems by Michigan State University, Trayak and ExxonMobil found polyethylene often had lower environmental impacts than paper, glass or metal. Replacing PE could raise greenhouse gas emissions three to four times, the authors say.

Read more at packaginginsights.com →

CSIR-NIIST develops a plant based bottle that breaks down in six months

The Tribune

Scientists at CSIR-NIIST in Thiruvananthapuram have developed a bottle from biodegradable polymers and plant derived materials that can biodegrade in about six months on land. It could hold water, juice, milk, cooking oil, personal care products and cleaning liquids.

Read more at The Tribune →

Vietnamese experts warn food packaging can leach substances

Vietnam.vn

Dr Tran Huy Hieu of Vietnam's National Institute of Food Safety and Hygiene Testing said packaging safety depends on impurities, decomposition products and substances generated during use, not only on what is added in production. He spoke at a 28 October workshop.

Read more at Vietnam.vn →

Review maps how farm waste could become bioplastics

Bioengineer.org

A review in Discover Polymers finds lignocellulosic biomass is the most scalable feedstock for biodegradable polymers, but high costs and limited infrastructure still block commercial use. Global plastic waste reached about 353 million tonnes in 2019, with only 9 per cent recycled.

Read more at Bioengineer.org →

Opinion: a student columnist says we should fear microplastics more

dailycampus.com

A UConn Daily Campus column argues microplastics now pervade soil, water and food, citing their detection in organs, blood and breast milk. The writer says the scale of contamination deserves far more public concern than it gets.

Read more at dailycampus.com →

EU plastic packaging waste rises faster than recycling

packaginginsights.com

Eurostat says each EU resident generated 35.9 kg of plastic packaging waste in 2024, of which 15.3 kg was recycled. The recycling rate rose slightly to 42.4 per cent, but experts warn gains are outpaced by growing plastic use.
