Delhi Master Plan 2047 explained, from 40 lakh homes to 200 km of cycle tracks

The plan that will shape how India's capital is built for the next two decades is now in its consultation season. Here is what it promises architects, planners and the people who will live inside it.

By Yes Bro Desk · 28 October 2026 · 3 min read

Master plans are the most important documents nobody reads. They decide where your office goes, how tall your building can be and whether you will cycle to work or sit in traffic thinking about cycling to work. Delhi has a new one, and this month it moved from the gazette to the meeting room.

What the plan actually says

Union Housing and Urban Affairs Minister Manohar Lal unveiled the Master Plan for Delhi 2047, or MPD 2047, in August, CNBC TV18 reported. It proposes around 40 lakh new homes, with a large focus on affordable housing, for a city that has a population of around 2.4 crore today. It also says Delhi's 520 sq km of agricultural land and 180 sq km of Ridge and forest must be protected.

So the city has to add a very large number of homes without spreading onto its farms or its forest. There is only one direction left, and it is up. The plan proposes redeveloping existing neighbourhoods and allowing denser development. Slums and JJ clusters are to be rehabilitated in the same areas under the "Jahan Jhuggi, Wahan Makan" principle, and unauthorised colonies get a framework for improvement, regularisation and redevelopment. Chief Minister Rekha Gupta said commercial activity will be allowed on the ground floors of buildings along 30 metre roads in residential areas.

The transport bet

The headline number is rail. The Metro, now 416 km, is planned to grow to 695 km, and the Namo Bharat regional network from 82 km to 383 km, The Indian Express reported from the Delhi Development Authority's eleventh stakeholder consultation last Friday. Transit hubs are proposed at Sarai Kale Khan, Anand Vihar and Kashmere Gate, with around 200 km of cycle tracks, safer pedestrian infrastructure and universal accessibility.

Two ideas deserve more attention than they will get. One is a Unified Metropolitan Transport Authority to bring every mode of transport under one roof. The other is a Unified Parking Planning Framework, which treats parking as a citywide management problem and proposes parking management zones in congested areas, Mint reported. Mint also noted the obvious. Implementation will depend on later planning, coordination among agencies and the approval of individual projects. A plan is a promise with a long delivery window.

Why architects should care

Denser redevelopment around transit is a design brief, not a slogan. It means mid rise housing that has to stay cool without a cooler in every room, streets that work for people on foot and ground floors that earn their keep. Heat is not a side issue here. A new study found India's green cover shrank 5.6 percentage points in 25 years, and green areas stayed 1°C to 2.5°C cooler than non green surfaces in summer, ET EnergyWorld reported. Protecting the Ridge is good. Shading the new streets matters just as much.

The market is already leaning green. Green certified buildings made up 80% of new office supply and 82% of leasing in the July to September quarter, according to CBRE's India office figures. Developers have noticed that tenants want efficient buildings. Housing will follow, if the rules nudge it.

The jobs in the plan

Plans like this create work for urban designers, landscape architects and people who can model energy and heat. Today's Yes Bro Architecture jobs list includes an Urban Designer role at AtkinsRéalis in Bengaluru and a Landscape Architect role at Surbana Jurong in Gurugram.

The skill that separates good planners from the rest is knowing how a street works before drawing it. That means talking to the vendor, the commuter and the resident who will live with the design. Earth5R's FIELDWORK fellowship is built around exactly that, hands-on fieldwork with real people, real conversations and real field research in Indian cities.

The verdict

MPD 2047 gets the big ideas right. Build up, not out. Put homes near trains. Protect the green. The hard part is the boring part, getting a dozen agencies to agree on one parking rule. The DDA's three month consultation is the place to start, and the people who turn up will shape the city for the next twenty years.
