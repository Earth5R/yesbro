A study from Saitama Medical Center links poor sleep quality during the first 24 hours after an elective cesarean delivery to higher postpartum depression scores. Researchers analyzed records from patients who underwent procedures under neuraxial anesthesia. The findings suggest a correlation between immediate postoperative sleep disruption and later outcomes.

A study at a primary HIV clinic in northern Vietnam suggests that integrating a structured sleep hygiene program into routine care is acceptable to patients and providers. Researchers identified sixteen facilitators and eleven barriers to implementation. The program focuses on behavioral practices to regulate sleep-wake cycles and bedroom environments.

Study links long sleep duration to heart disease mortality

A study of 3,287 American adults with heart disease suggests that sleeping nine hours or more daily is associated with a higher risk of death. Published in PLOS One, the research compared mortality rates across three sleep duration groups. Extra sleep may signal an underlying health problem.

MIT study links brain waste clearance to aging

The Boston Globe

Researchers at MIT found that the process of flushing waste from the brain during sleep is commonly impaired in older adults. Participants aged 60 to 84 showed significantly smaller waves of cerebrospinal fluid compared to younger adults. The fluid is responsible for washing away toxic, memory-impairing proteins.
