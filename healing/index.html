Mental Health

Social media use ranks among the top predictors of depression in a study of 183,000 adults

psypost.org

Researchers used machine learning on surveys of more than 183,000 American adults from 2014 to 2024 and found social media use among the strongest predictors of self-reported depression, PsyPost reports. Its contribution to risk rose past about two and a half hours a day, and the link held beyond teenagers.

Read more at psypost.org →

Getting a smartphone by 12 was linked to higher odds of depression in a study of 10,588 children

sheknows.com

A study in Pediatrics, led by Children's Hospital of Philadelphia researchers using the ABCD Study, found that owning a smartphone by age 12 was linked with higher odds of depression, obesity and insufficient sleep, SheKnows reports. The study does not show that the phone causes them.

Read more at sheknows.com →

Feeling lonely may matter more than how often you see people

The Economic Times

A study of 3,082 older adults followed for up to 10 years found that those who reported more loneliness declined faster in memory and thinking skills, The Economic Times reports. The research, in The Journals of Gerontology, suggests feeling lonely is different from simply having fewer social contacts.

Read more at The Economic Times →

A psychiatrist describes the quiet depression that keeps people working

NPR

Psychiatrist Judith Joseph tells NPR that some people with signs of depression never stop moving, keep meeting obligations at work and home, and quietly lose the ability to feel pleasure. She calls it high-functioning depression and says it often goes unnoticed, even by the people living it.

Read more at NPR →

Nearly half of young remote workers say working from home makes them lonely

nz.news.yahoo.com

The Bupa Wellbeing Index found 47 per cent of 18 to 24 year olds feel remote working causes loneliness, up from 42 per cent in 2025, Yahoo News reports. Across all adults the figure fell slightly to 34 per cent. The commute, it seems, had a social life.

Read more at nz.news.yahoo.com →
More from Mental Health

Chatbots may mirror and amplify the feelings of lonely young adults

ls.wisc.edu

University of Wisconsin researchers Catalina Toma and Jinyoung Choi studied around 100 lonely college students and found that AI chatbot replies mirror and amplify the emotions users bring to them, in turn shaping their loneliness. The researchers say the effect could cut either way.

Read more at ls.wisc.edu →

A doctor-led CBT skills group eased anxiety and depression in primary care

Bioengineer.org

An eight-week cognitive behavioural therapy skills group run by physicians was linked with meaningful drops in anxiety and depression symptoms at three months, according to an observational study in BMC Primary Care. The authors call results beyond three months exploratory because of missing follow-up data.

Read more at Bioengineer.org →

Is a teenager depressed or just lazy

news.llu.edu

Loma Linda University experts say not all teenage laziness is laziness, and that changes in behaviour can be normal but may also signal depression. They urge parents to look at the story behind the behaviour, and note teen mental health rates have improved since the pandemic peak without fully recovering.

Read more at news.llu.edu →

A guide to psychological first aid for everyday emotional crises

ijpr.org

In The Johns Hopkins Guide to Everyday Psychological First Aid, George Everly Jr. and Jeffrey Lating argue that homes and workplaces should be ready to handle emotional distress the way they keep first aid kits for injuries. They note about one in three adults will face a fear-related disorder.

Read more at ijpr.org →

Good money habits are linked with better mental health

money.com

A University of South Australia study of more than 17,000 people found those who saved regularly and paid credit cards on time tended to report better mental health, more energy and closer relationships, Money reports. The headline says the study "proves" it. The data shows a link.

Read more at money.com →

Chhattisgarh trains educators to support children's mental health

The New Indian Express

Chhattisgarh's education department, with UNICEF and the Tata Institute of Social Sciences, has launched an initiative to strengthen mental health and psychosocial support in schools, The New Indian Express reports. A three-day programme under Samagra Shiksha drew on TISS's school mental health advocacy work.

Read more at The New Indian Express →

Kit de Waal says books pulled her back from a breakdown

BBC News

Bestselling author Kit de Waal told the BBC she left home at 16, struggled with drugs and was "a couple of months away from a very serious breakdown" when a solicitor recommended some reading. "I can honestly say it saved my life," she said.

Read more at BBC News →

Mindfulness and Meditation

Did Silicon Valley rename yoga nidra as NSDR

Mint

Non-Sleep Deep Rest, a term coined by neuroscientist Andrew Huberman in 2021, involves lying still with eyes closed while a guided audio cues breath and body awareness, Mint notes. Yoga nidra has asked people to do much the same for decades. The acronym is newer.

Read more at Mint →

Gratitude practices do not help everyone in the same way

futurity.org

A study in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences finds the effects of gratitude interventions, from journaling to writing a letter of thanks, differ across countries and cultures and by the type of practice, Futurity reports. Counting blessings, it turns out, depends on who is counting.

Read more at futurity.org →

Two yoga students describe steadier sleep through practice

voiceofasean.com

Voice of ASEAN profiles two students at a yoga school in Taytay in the Philippines, one prescribed sleep medication during mental health treatment and one who worked night shifts for 15 years, who say yoga helped their sleep. The school stresses yoga supports, but never replaces, medical care.

Read more at voiceofasean.com →

Thirteen happiness researchers compare notes on the good life

upworthy.com

Thirteen leading wellbeing researchers met on a Zoom call to share what a combined 600 years of research had taught them about a good life, Upworthy reports. The backdrop is a 2025 Gallup poll in which only 44 percent of Americans said they were very satisfied with their lives.

Read more at upworthy.com →

Ira Trivedi shows simple yoga for back pain and stress

India Today

Author and yoga teacher Ira Trivedi demonstrates warm-ups, spine rotations, neck exercises and poses such as Bhujangasana, Shashankasana and cat and cow for desk stiffness, back pain and mental stress in an India Today session. She also covers breathing techniques.

Read more at India Today →

Sleep and Balance

What permanent daylight saving time could mean for sleep and mood

psypost.org

A policy analysis in CMAJ argues permanent standard time would better support winter sleep, mood and body rhythms than permanent daylight saving time, PsyPost reports. A review on mental illness agrees, while a population review finds clock changes bring a mix of harms and benefits.

Read more at psypost.org →

Emotional memories may decide whether you stay asleep

mindbodygreen.com

A study in Science identified a biological mechanism showing that emotionally charged memories do not just follow people into sleep but help determine whether they stay asleep, mindbodygreen reports. It explains the familiar experience of waking and replaying a hard conversation at night.

Read more at mindbodygreen.com →

Sleep quality, more than length, links work stress to low mood

Bioengineer.org

A cross-sectional study of 9,736 workers in China's Gansu Province found that sleep quality and disturbance accounted for most of the statistical link between work stress and depressive symptoms, while sleep length mattered less, according to BMC Public Health. It shows association, not cause.

Read more at Bioengineer.org →

Anxious personalities report less sleep, but wrist monitors disagree

psypost.org

Villanova researchers studied 134 undergraduates and found those higher in neuroticism reported sleeping less, while wrist-worn monitors did not show they actually slept less, according to a study in Personality and Individual Differences. The worry, in other words, extended to the sleep diary.

Read more at psypost.org →

A large MRI study ties sleep efficiency to belly fat

longevity.technology

Whole-body MRI company Prenuvo and UT Dallas sleep scientists including Matthew Walker analysed scans of 14,031 adults and linked sleep efficiency to visceral fat and brain volume, Longevity.Technology reports. People reporting more than eight hours of sleep had smaller hippocampi, one of the odder findings.

Read more at longevity.technology →
More from Sleep and Balance

Short sleep after a stroke is linked to higher depression risk

Bioengineer.org

A meta-analysis of six observational studies covering 5,624 cases found stroke survivors who sleep too little had nearly double the odds of post-stroke depression, according to Frontiers in Neurology. The authors pooled observational data, so the link does not prove cause.

Read more at Bioengineer.org →

How to tell if you have seasonal affective disorder

NPR

NPR's Life Kit revisits seasonal affective disorder, first described by psychiatrist Norman Rosenthal in 1984 after he noticed people becoming "depressed on schedule" in winter. It says about 1 in 20 people are affected and explains what can help. The story was first published in 2020 and updated.

Read more at NPR →

Body and Movement

Brisk walking beat casual walking for reversing frailty

fitandwell.com

In a 16-week study of prefrail and frail adults doing three supervised walks a week, walking hard enough to reach 70% of maximum heart rate improved frailty more than casual walking, Fit and Well reports. Both speeds improved one frailty measure.

Read more at fitandwell.com →

Blood flow restriction training built leg strength in older adults

Bioengineer.org

A review of six randomised trials with 197 people aged 60 and older found low-load exercise with an inflatable cuff improved leg strength and muscle size about as well as heavy training, according to the European Review of Aging and Physical Activity. It suits people who cannot lift heavy.

Read more at Bioengineer.org →

Strength training tips for your 40s, 50s and 60s

USA Today

USA Today gathers strength advice for midlife and beyond, with UCLA sports medicine physician Emily Miller saying "strength training improves physical function and independence." The core idea is regularly asking muscles to work a little harder, rather than buying supplements or expensive treatments.

Read more at USA Today →

Fifteen minutes of cycling twice a week helped adults over 70

Todo Mountain Bike

A study in Frontiers in Sports and Active Living tested low-intensity stationary cycling combined with blood flow restriction in adults over 70 and found benefits for strength and mobility, Todo Mountain Bike reports. The sessions were 15 minutes, twice a week.

Read more at Todo Mountain Bike →

Teenagers who practise judo report less school stress

bjjdoc.com

A study of 553 Polish students aged 10 to 15 found 43.8% of judo practitioners rarely or never felt school stress, against 25.3% of peers who did no organised sport, BJJDOC reports. Young judokas were also more likely to say they always feel safe at school.

Read more at bjjdoc.com →

A 90-year-old's routine of gym sessions and three daily walks

health.chosun.com

Tom Conroy, 90, who lives on the Keele University campus with his wife, spends an hour and a half in the gym every day and takes three walks of about 50 minutes each, the BBC reported. He began exercising as a child in his father's boxing gym.

How to actually start exercising

bodyandsoul.news.com.au

Body and Soul asked top trainers how to begin exercising when the benefits are clear but the first step is not. The advice covers the nerves of joining a gym or trying a new sport and how to work around common fitness setbacks. Everyone has been a beginner.

Fitness, health and performance are not the same thing

runnersworld.com

Runner's World asked two exercise physiologists to separate health, fitness and performance, three measures runners often treat as interchangeable. The piece explains what each means and how to tell which one your training is actually targeting.

Hindustan Times

Former India cricket captain MS Dhoni urged Gen Z and Gen Alpha to make physical activity and sport a priority, speaking at an event alongside lyricist Javed Akhtar, Hindustan Times reports. He pointed to changing food habits and sedentary lifestyles as the problem.

Food and Nutrition

Coffee and decaf both shifted gut microbes and mood in a small study

news.ssbcrack.com

University College Cork researchers had 62 healthy adults, including regular coffee drinkers who quit for two weeks and then resumed with caffeinated or decaf coffee, and found links to changes in gut microbes, mood and cognition, SSBCrack News reports. Decaf had effects too.

Read more at news.ssbcrack.com →

Regular tea drinking was linked to lower heart disease risk

prevention.com

A new study links regular tea drinking to an 11% lower risk of heart disease, with the greatest benefit at six to eight cups a day, Prevention reports. Tea combined with high physical activity and not smoking showed the largest reduction. The study shows a link, not a cause.

Read more at prevention.com →

Multivitamins raise vitamin levels, but health benefits are mixed

News-Medical

A scoping review of clinical trials in the Journal of Dietary Supplements found multivitamins most consistently raised blood folate, vitamin B6 and B12, while iron, zinc and iodine helped only in some contexts, News-Medical reports. Effects varied by formula, population and length of use.

Read more at News-Medical →

Fish oil did not give a major memory boost in a new study

Hindustan Times

Researchers at Keck Medicine of USC, writing in eBioMedicine, found participants who took omega-3 supplements daily saw no major cognitive advantage, Hindustan Times reports. The result challenges a common reason people buy fish oil capsules.

Read more at Hindustan Times →

Which supplements are worth the money

health.yahoo.com

With the US supplements industry worth about $70 billion a year and eight in 10 people taking them, experts weigh in on what is useful, Yahoo Health reports. "People have kitchen cabinets that look like GNC warehouses," says Sanjiv Lal of Regen Therapy, who thinks supplementation is overcomplicated.

Read more at health.yahoo.com →

An IVF specialist on fertility supplements and expensive hype

Hindustan Times

From conception gummies to herbal blends, fertility supplements are having a moment online, Hindustan Times reports. An IVF specialist says supplements should fill specific gaps rather than promise a shortcut, and that diet, sleep, exercise and stress control are what support fertility.

Read more at Hindustan Times →

Teaching teens to manage emotions helped them keep weight off

gilmorehealth.com

A randomised trial of 166 adolescents and their parents found adding emotional skills training to family-based weight treatment helped teens keep more weight off, Gilmore Health reports. A year later, 22% in the combined group kept a meaningful reduction, against 4% with standard treatment.

Read more at gilmorehealth.com →

BMI can miss malnutrition in older adults

mindbodygreen.com

Researchers assessed 303 adults aged 65 and older in Northern Italy and found malnutrition, muscle loss and metabolic problems often overlapped, with nearly one in three malnourished adults overweight or obese, mindbodygreen reports. Body weight alone, the study suggests, does not show muscle or nutrient status.

Read more at mindbodygreen.com →

Three litres of water a day will not guarantee glowing skin

Hindustan Times

Celebrities including Kareena Kapoor credit water for their skin, but dermatologist Swetha Sridhar tells Hindustan Times that drinking three litres a day does not guarantee a glow. The piece explains what hydration actually does for skin. The internet has been asked to adjust.

Read more at Hindustan Times →

Why diet and exercise work best together for weight loss

Hindustan Times

Dr Neha Shah tells Hindustan Times that weight loss plans which favour diet over exercise, or the reverse, can backfire, and that both need to work together. The piece looks at the common pattern of exercising hard and then ordering food afterwards.

Read more at Hindustan Times →

Screen-time eating linked to a double burden of malnutrition in Saudi students

Bioengineer.org

A study of 268 middle school students in Saudi Arabia found underweight and overweight or obesity at nearly identical levels in the same school, with 49.8% having an abnormal BMI for age, Bioengineer reports. The researchers linked eating in front of screens to the pattern.

Longevity

A Weizmann Institute study links genes to up to 55% of lifespan

dongascience.com

Research from the Weizmann Institute suggests genes may contribute as much as 55% of natural human lifespan, DongA Science reports, raising the old question of how much healthy habits can change. The report also notes scientists have proposed a theoretical lifespan limit of around 150 years.

Read more at dongascience.com →

Having a parent who lived to 100 was linked to lower health risks

thestar.com.my

A JAMA Network Open study pooling the LonGenity study, the New England Centenarian Study and UK Biobank compared 2,319 adults who had a parent live to 100 with 2,703 others. They had a much lower risk of death, heart disease and high blood pressure, The Star reports.

Read more at thestar.com.my →

Blocking one enzyme extended lifespan in ageing mice by 17%

ScienceAlert

Researchers in China found that blocking asparagine endopeptidase, an enzyme that becomes more active with age and has been linked to Alzheimer's disease and DNA damage, extended the lifespan of ageing mice by 17%, ScienceAlert reports. The work was in mice.

Read more at ScienceAlert →

The most cheerful nuns in a classic study lived longer

futura-sciences.com

In the Nun Study, 180 American nuns wrote short autobiographies at about age 22, and decades later University of Kentucky researchers found the most cheerful lived up to 10 years longer, Futura reports. The article stresses the findings come with important limitations.

Read more at futura-sciences.com →

A new metabolic marker aims to predict healthy ageing

eurekalert.org

European researchers presented a personalised indicator that analyses the chemicals the body produces to give a detailed snapshot of metabolism and predict longevity and chronic disease risk, EurekAlert reports. They note about 60% of people over 65 live with two or more chronic conditions.

Read more at eurekalert.org →

What longevity influencers get wrong about women

health.yahoo.com

Yoga therapy showed early signals for heart rate variability after cancer

Bioengineer.org

A small, unblinded early-phase study of 20 cancer survivors, average age 55.7, reported mixed but intriguing signs that structured yoga therapy may shift heart rate variability, according to BioPsychoSocial Medicine. The researchers stress it was designed to spot a signal, not to show that yoga therapy works.

Gentle ear nerve stimulation was tested for high blood pressure

labroots.com

A randomised trial in Hypertension gave 63 people with uncontrolled high blood pressure, all already on medication, either a noninvasive ear nerve stimulation device or a sham for 12 weeks, Labroots reports. The authors suggest it could be a safe, accessible add-on.

It was announced in New Delhi on October 29 alongside an intellectual property facility for Ayurveda.
