Mental Health

One dose of a psychedelic nasal spray eased hard to treat depression in a trial

health.yahoo.com

A JAMA trial at 38 sites gave 193 adults whose depression had not improved after two to five antidepressants a single nasal spray of mebufotenin, or 5-MeO-DMT, which reduced symptoms, Yahoo Health reports. Its effects peak within 15 minutes and usually fade within an hour.

Read more at health.yahoo.com →

Researchers propose circadian depression as a possible subtype

National Geographic

A study in the British Journal of Psychiatry by Australian researchers suggests that for some people, staying up late, sleeping long and waking exhausted may mark a distinct form of depression driven by a disrupted body clock, National Geographic reports. The subtype is still a proposal.

Read more at National Geographic →

Aparna Piramal Raje on living a full life with bipolar disorder

The Better India

Writer and bestselling author Aparna Piramal Raje has lived with bipolar disorder for over two decades. In a piece published in partnership with the India Mental Health Alliance, she shares what helps her, from her family's love to a sleep routine she treats as sacred.

Read more at The Better India →

A weakened brain signal may link several schizophrenia symptoms

ScienceDaily

McGill University scientists identified a weakened brain signal that may connect seemingly unrelated symptoms of schizophrenia, including sensory processing difficulties and disorganised speech, ScienceDaily reports. They say it could help explain the condition and potentially lead to earlier detection.

Read more at ScienceDaily →

Northern Ireland police will hand more mental health calls to health staff

BBC News

The PSNI handles about 30,000 mental health calls a year. From November it will stop welfare checks unless there is a risk to life, harm or a crime, and handlers may pass calls to health and social care staff, the BBC reports. Reaction has been mixed.

Read more at BBC News →
More from Mental Health

A psychologist argues emotion dysregulation sits at the root of ADHD

psychologytoday.com

Writing in Psychology Today, a clinician argues that research points to emotion dysregulation as a root of ADHD, which may explain the attention symptoms and why ADHD often appears with other conditions. Focusing on it, the piece says, could guide more effective treatment.

Read more at psychologytoday.com →

One clown visit nearly halved sadness scores in adult hospital patients

inkl.com

In Colombia, 40 adults on an internal medicine ward each got one 20 minute visit from the same pair of healthcare clowns. Their average sadness score fell by almost half, and anxiety and anger dropped too. Forty patients is a small study, and scores were taken right afterwards.

Read more at inkl.com →

What the evidence says about creatine and mental health

inkl.com

Creatine's benefits for strength are well supported, and it is increasingly being recommended for mental health, with a recent review suggesting possible benefits as a depression treatment, a piece republished by inkl reports. The evidence for mood is newer than the evidence for muscle.

Read more at inkl.com →

Meta settles a teen's social media mental health claim before trial

koreatimes.co.kr

Meta settled with a teen plaintiff who said Instagram and Facebook were designed to be addictive and harmed her mental health, her attorneys said on Friday. Meta had settled with a second teen days earlier, so the trial is expected to proceed against Snap only.

Jagruti, which describes itself as one of India's largest organised mental health, addiction and dementia care platforms, has raised Rs 28 crore in its first institutional round, led by Alkemi Growth Capital, BW Disrupt reports.

Punjab's health department has told mental health establishments to register with the State Mental Health Authority under the Mental Healthcare Act, 2017, warning that failure to comply may invite action, the Times of India reports. The aim is effective implementation of the Act.

Mindfulness and Meditation

Meditation and VR training led to more lucid dreams in a small study

ua.news

University of Trento researchers trained students with meditation, reality checks and virtual reality scenes where objects broke the laws of physics. Doing this twice a week for a month led to lucid dreams four times as often as reality checks alone, Live Science reports.

Read more at ua.news →

Personalised music with beats lowered cortisol more than pink noise

Medical Xpress

A randomised trial at Toronto Metropolitan University found 24 minutes of personalised music paired with auditory beat stimulation lowered the stress hormone cortisol more than twice as much as pink noise, a popular relaxation sound, Medical Xpress reports.

Read more at Medical Xpress →

Twenty minutes of music may ease pain and low mood, a review finds

express.co.uk

A BMC Psychology meta-analysis of nine randomised trials in 787 patients with pain found 20 minutes of music can help relieve pain and associated depression symptoms, the Express reports. It worked best when patients chose their own music.

Read more at express.co.uk →

People remember a hug for how it made them feel

The Times of India

A PLOS One study of 15,049 UK adults led by the University of Bristol found people were more likely to remember a hug because it made them feel comforted, supported or understood than because of the gesture itself, the Times of India reports.

Read more at The Times of India →

Sky and trees help campus stress recovery, up to a point

Bioengineer.org

Researchers in Nanning, China, paired over a thousand campus walkway photos with students' ratings and found the restorative effect of greenery did not keep rising with more green in view, and open sky had limits too, in Computational Urban Science.

Read more at Bioengineer.org →

Sleep and Balance

Healthy sleep duration linked to a younger biological age

news.ssbcrack.com

A Nature study using data from about half a million adults in the MULTI Consortium examined sleep duration and biological age gaps, the difference between how old the body seems and how old it is, SSBCrack reports. The study shows associations.

Read more at news.ssbcrack.com →

For menopausal insomnia, CBT-I held up against pills in a review

psychiatrictimes.com

A systematic review found non drug approaches were robust first line options for chronic insomnia in peri and postmenopausal women, Psychiatric Times reports. Indirect comparisons suggested CBT-I works about as well as Z drugs, contrary to what the investigators expected.

Read more at psychiatrictimes.com →

How sleep changes as we age

stacker.com

As the body clock shifts with age, sleep often becomes lighter and more interrupted, according to the US National Institute on Aging, a Stacker piece from BetterSleep explains. That does not mean the need for rest disappears.

Read more at stacker.com →

One in four online reviewers stopped a newer insomnia drug

News-Medical

Rutgers researchers analysed hundreds of online reviews of the prescription insomnia drug lemborexant and found about one in four reviewers stopped or planned to stop taking it, News-Medical reports. Insomnia affects about 30 per cent of adults.

Read more at News-Medical →

In Bupa's Health or Hype video series, fitness advocate Joe Wicks cut his sleep to about five hours a night for three nights while sleep specialist Dr Sophie Bostock watched. His screen time passed 13.5 hours a day and carbohydrate cravings rose.

An editorial in Frontiers in Nutrition argues that nutrition research should stop treating diet, sleep, activity and wellbeing as separate variables, since eating habits rarely change in isolation, Bioengineer reports.

A retrospective study from Tampere University Hospital in Finland found children with sleep disordered breathing who had only their adenoids removed had far fewer complications than those who also had tonsils removed, though they needed repeat surgery more often.

Body and Movement

A hot bath lowered blood pressure more than a bike workout in a small study

verywellhealth.com

In a small study, 30 minutes in a hot bath lowered blood pressure more than 30 minutes of cycling, Verywell Health reports. Experts say baths should not replace exercise but may help people who cannot exercise regularly, and some should check with a doctor first.

Read more at verywellhealth.com →

Chronic back pain may become tied to the brain's sense of self

ScienceDaily

UC San Diego researchers found movement triggered pain in chronic lower back pain activates a brain network linked to self awareness and memories, which ordinary acute pain usually switches off. It may help explain why painkillers often give limited relief, ScienceDaily reports.

Read more at ScienceDaily →

Elastic band training improved balance in older adults with sarcopenia

Bioengineer.org

A 12 week elastic band programme improved functional performance, balance and gait symmetry in 120 older adults with sarcopenia, in a single arm study from Jilin Sport University published in Frontiers in Physiology. With no control group, the gains are hard to attribute.

Read more at Bioengineer.org →

Overtraining kept dopamine high in rest periods in mice

Mirage News

Brazilian researchers writing in Neuroscience found mice on an exhaustive exercise routine had dopamine in the hippocampus stay elevated when they should have been resting. If confirmed in people, it could help explain anxiety and insomnia in overtrained athletes.

Read more at Mirage News →

Three weekly band sessions beat two for strength in women around 75

Bioengineer.org

A randomised trial found 12 weeks of low load elastic band training improved fitness in women averaging 75 whether they trained twice or three times a week, but the three session group gained markedly more grip strength and HDL cholesterol.

Read more at Bioengineer.org →

A randomised trial of 79 adults at a rehabilitation centre in Bangladesh found adding upper and mid thoracic spine mobilisation to standard physiotherapy reduced mechanical neck pain and disability more than physiotherapy alone.

How long to walk for blood sugar and blood pressure

verywellhealth.com

A 10 minute walk after eating can help lower post meal blood sugar spikes, and walking 20 to 40 minutes three to five times a week may help lower blood pressure, Verywell Health reports. The usual target is 150 minutes of moderate exercise a week.

University of Basel psychologist Dr Lena Glaser asked study participants to take cold showers, to test whether people adopt climate friendly habits that also offer personal rewards. Those who stuck with the routine reported several benefits, News-Medical reports.

Diet and exercise put type 2 diabetes into remission for over half of younger adults

ScienceDaily

In a trial reported by the European Association for the Study of Diabetes, 54 per cent of participants under 45 on an intensive low calorie diet and structured exercise programme reached remission in six months, against 4 per cent on usual care.

Read more at ScienceDaily →

Vegans had lower gut bacterial diversity in a Danish study

News-Medical

University of Copenhagen researchers studying 75 vegans and 75 omnivores expected vegans to have the healthiest gut microbiome. Diversity was lower on average among vegans, News-Medical reports. The researchers called it a major surprise.

Read more at News-Medical →

Animal protein linked to lower testosterone in Finnish men

nutritioninsight.com

In 2,546 Finnish men aged 42 to 60, more animal protein was linked to lower total testosterone and more plant protein to higher levels, Nutrition Insight reports. The differences were small, and the cross sectional design cannot establish causation.

Read more at nutritioninsight.com →

Peels and stalks may be the fibre most people are missing

BBC News

UK researchers say fruit and vegetable scraps such as peels and stalks are an overlooked source of dietary fibre, and encourage root to tip eating, including watermelon rind and pineapple core, the BBC reports. The bin, it turns out, was the pantry.

Regular tea drinking linked to lower vascular disease risk

mirror.co.uk

A study in the Journal of Internal Medicine followed 485,934 adults in China for just over nine years and suggests regular tea drinkers may have a lower risk of vascular disease, the Mirror reports. The study is observational.

Read more at mirror.co.uk →

Adults who received plant based grocery packages from a Boston area food pantry were in better overall health after a couple of years, researchers report in Preventing Chronic Disease. The study involved 68 adults, and the authors say longer programmes are needed.

A cross sectional study in the British Journal of Nutrition examined how UK adults' expected feelings about foods, from chocolate to roast dinners, relate to diet quality and comfort eating, News-Medical reports. It also looked at how branding relates to those expectations.

A healthy diet linked to lower heart risk in cancer survivors

A study in JACC: Advances found several healthy eating patterns were associated with better cardiovascular outcomes and longer life expectancy among cancer survivors, for whom heart disease is a leading cause of non cancer death, Medical Dialogues reports.

The Planetary Health Diet scored well on nutrition in a Turkish study

mindbodygreen.com

Researchers assessing 300 adults at a diet clinic in Turkey found closer adherence to the plant forward Planetary Health Diet was linked to better diet quality and perhaps a smaller environmental footprint, MindBodyGreen reports. It does not require cutting out meat or dairy.

Stress and workdays may undo healthy eating plans

mindbodygreen.com

A mixed methods study of 232 university employees in Saudi Arabia examined how dietary habits, nutrition knowledge and stress relate, MindBodyGreen reports. The magazine says the findings challenge common assumptions about why people struggle to eat well at work.

An ATREE study documented 75 beetle species eaten by Indigenous communities across Northeast India. Of these, 24 are reported as edible for the first time anywhere and five are new records of edible use in India, the New Indian Express reports.
