Mental Health

Talking about mental health more has not meant more people seeking help

India Today

At India Today's HealthWealth Summit, psychiatrist Dr Samir Parikh said about three quarters of people with mental health difficulties still do not seek help. He added that the share reaching professionals has barely moved in 25 to 30 years, despite far more open conversation.

Read more at India Today →

Many women treated for anxiety or depression also have unrecognised ADHD

psypost.org

A study in The International Journal of Psychiatry in Medicine found nearly one in five women seeking treatment for anxiety or depression also had ADHD, and almost half of those cases had never been diagnosed. Overlapping symptoms can hide the attention condition in adult women, researchers say.

Read more at psypost.org →

People caring for someone with cancer report far more mental distress

curetoday.com

A national survey of about 200,000 US adults, published in Supportive Care in Cancer, found people caring for a loved one with cancer had 89 per cent higher odds of frequent mental distress than non caregivers. The study points to caregivers needing support of their own.

Read more at curetoday.com →

Loneliness and poor health in later life feed each other, study finds

Bioengineer.org

Using European survey data on adults aged 50 and over, researchers writing in the European Journal of Ageing examined whether loneliness predicts declining mental, cognitive and functional health over time, or the reverse. Their findings suggest loneliness may be both a cause and a consequence of poorer health.

Read more at Bioengineer.org →

An acceptance based programme may help people through career setbacks

Mirage News

A randomised trial in the Journal of Counseling Psychology tested an intervention based on acceptance and commitment therapy for people facing job loss or career uncertainty. Researchers found it helped participants handle work challenges while improving wellbeing and reducing psychological distress, and made them more psychologically flexible.

Read more at Mirage News →
More from Mental Health

About half of people with Behçet's disease live with depression or anxiety

Bioengineer.org

A meta-analysis in Advances in Rheumatology estimated that about 47 per cent of patients with Behçet's disease, a chronic inflammatory condition of the blood vessels, experience depression and about 48 per cent experience anxiety. Authors say mental health care should be part of routine treatment.

Read more at Bioengineer.org →

Researchers build a chatbot that plans each reply using therapy skills

Bioengineer.org

A team writing in BMC Artificial Intelligence built DBT-wellMind-GameTree, a chatbot that mixes language models, game theory and dialectical behaviour therapy to choose its approach before each reply to stressed students. In testing it scored higher than ChatGPT and Gemini baselines on goals, satisfaction and coping.

Read more at Bioengineer.org →

Northern Ireland trims a crisis scheme for young people in A and E

BBC News

The Western Health and Social Care Trust has reduced its Card Before You Leave scheme, which gives young people in mental health crisis a written specialist appointment before leaving the emergency department. Citing financial pressures, the trust now runs it only on weekdays, raising concern among families and campaigners.

Read more at BBC News →

Low mood alongside heart and metabolic illness raises frailty risk in older adults

Bioengineer.org

A study of 5,513 Chinese adults aged 45 and over, published in Diabetology and Metabolic Syndrome, found those with both advanced cardiovascular kidney metabolic syndrome and depressive symptoms had more than three times the risk of frailty, compared with people who had neither condition.

Read more at Bioengineer.org →

Finding calm in how small we are

mindful.org

Writing for Mindful, an author typing in a grey North London café looks up and notices that every person around her carries a life as complicated as her own. The piece describes how feeling small among others can ease the weight of personal worries.

A college student finds steadiness in a campus meditation group

miscellanynews.org

A Vassar College student describes joining the campus Buddhist Sangha during a hectic first six weeks of college, with little prior experience of meditation. Sangha roughly means community, and she writes that sitting with others helped her stay grounded amid new people and activities.

Read more at miscellanynews.org →

A coach on working through painful memories with forgiveness

discoverestevan.com

On Discover Estevan's Wellness Weekly, life coach Stacey Wempe says processing painful memories starts with naming the feelings involved and talking with someone supportive. She urges people to avoid self blame and shame, and recommends a counsellor or professional for trauma or PTSD.

How well do children's mindfulness questionnaires actually work?

pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

A study in the Journal of Experimental Child Psychology examined self report measures of mindfulness used with children. Researchers tested how well the questionnaires match other measures and predict outcomes, and how much children's wish to give good answers may skew their scores.

Sleep and Balance

Sleep problems add little to dementia risk models in one patient group

Bioengineer.org

A cross sectional study in Ageing International looked at insomnia severity, sleeping pill doses and sleep apnoea risk in older adults. Once well known dementia risk factors were counted, these sleep measures added surprisingly little power to explain thinking problems in the group studied.

Read more at Bioengineer.org →

Samsung brings upgraded sleep apnoea detection to more Galaxy wearables

9to5Google

Samsung says an improved AI sleep apnoea detection feature, which analyses breathing disturbances per hour, arrives on selected Galaxy wearables in November. The original version launched in 2024 after US FDA approval, flagging possible breathing interruptions so users could seek medical advice.

Read more at 9to5Google →

A writer tries feng shui in the bedroom and sleeps better

vogueadria.com

Writing in Vogue Adria, Violeta Valdés describes rearranging her bedroom by feng shui principles, including a headboard, soft neutral colours, no clutter under the bed and devices kept away from her head. She says the changes helped her sleep. It is one person's experience.

Read more at vogueadria.com →

Babies' sleep habits may begin forming in the womb

Bioengineer.org

A study in Nature Communications monitored fetal activity for at least five days during the third trimester. Fetuses with a stronger preference for night time sleep tended to keep that preference after birth, pointing to a possible prenatal start to early sleep patterns.

Read more at Bioengineer.org →

More bright daylight is linked to lower dementia risk

ScienceDaily

In a study of more than 87,000 adults reported by ScienceDaily, higher daytime light exposure was associated with a 16 per cent lower risk of dementia. Getting less than 42 minutes of bright daylight a day was a stronger predictor than several established risk factors.

Read more at ScienceDaily →

Body and Movement

Squats first or jumps first? It barely matters, a trial finds

Bioengineer.org

In an eight week randomised trial of 24 resistance trained men, doing strength work before jump training or the reverse produced nearly identical gains in strength, jumping, sprinting and change of direction, as long as total workload stayed the same.

Read more at Bioengineer.org →

Staying flexible may help you age well

sciencefocus.com

BBC Science Focus reports on research linking flexibility to healthy ageing, including a study that tested middle aged people's range of motion across major joints and followed them for about 13 years. Greater flexibility was linked to lower risk of premature death, especially in women.

Read more at sciencefocus.com →

Short bursts of exercise worked as well as one longer session

life.liga.net

In an eight week study in the Journal of Exercise Science and Fitness, 45 students aged 18 to 25 followed a squat programme either in brief one minute bursts spread through the day or as one full workout. Strength gains were similar in both groups.

Read more at life.liga.net →

Twenty minutes of cycling intervals improved fitness in older women

todomountainbike.net

A study in Experimental Physiology found three 20 minute high intensity cycling sessions a week for six weeks produced measurable metabolic and cardiovascular improvements in 20 active postmenopausal women, average age 66.6, without a big rise in weekly training time.

Read more at todomountainbike.net →

Trainer Caroline Idiens, 54, shares a light weight shoulder workout

womenshealthmag.com

Women's Health shares a six move shoulder routine from trainer Caroline Idiens using light weights. The piece notes muscle loss can begin in the 30s and that only 4.3 per cent of women aged 19 to 65 met twice weekly strength guidance in one survey.

Read more at womenshealthmag.com →

A two minute weekly hold may help keep blood pressure down

The Economic Times

A study in BMJ Open Sport and Exercise Medicine suggests a single weekly session of isometric exercise, such as wall squats, planks or hand grips, may help people keep improvements in blood pressure gained from earlier training.

Read more at The Economic Times →

Twelve weeks of massage eased knee osteoarthritis pain in a trial

Bioengineer.org

A randomised trial of 56 people with medial knee osteoarthritis, published in Aging Clinical and Experimental Research, found twice weekly massage for 12 weeks improved pain, stiffness and physical function more than health education sessions. Assessors did not know which group participants were in.

Read more at Bioengineer.org →

A doctor clears up common myths about joint health

Hindustan Times

Speaking to HT Lifestyle, Dr Ashish Baweja tackles popular joint health myths, saying there is little evidence that cracking knuckles directly causes arthritis. He explains that habits formed when young shape joint health later, and that ignoring persistent pain is a mistake.

Read more at Hindustan Times →

Marathon runners can drink too much water, a doctor warns

Hindustan Times

Dr Judhajit Sengupta of Narayana RN Tagore Hospital in Kolkata tells HT Lifestyle that drinking far more than the body needs during a marathon can lower blood sodium, a condition called exercise associated hyponatraemia. Its signs can resemble dehydration, so runners should know the difference.

Read more at Hindustan Times →

A trial tests resistance training for people with Parkinson's

parkinsonsnewstoday.com

A new clinical trial is enrolling people with Parkinson's for supervised resistance training. Researchers want to learn how movement speed thresholds affect fatigue and muscle adaptation, and to find exercise plans that help strength, mobility and independence in daily life.

Read more at parkinsonsnewstoday.com →

Food and Nutrition

Some sugar substitutes are linked to faster brain ageing

ScienceDaily

A study of nearly 13,000 adults from the American Academy of Neurology found those consuming the most artificial sweeteners had a 62 per cent faster decline in memory and thinking, about 1.6 extra years of brain ageing. The study did not establish cause.

Read more at ScienceDaily →

Daily fibre may ease osteoarthritis pain, a small trial suggests

nypost.com

A University of Nottingham clinical trial found daily fibre supplements were linked to less pain, better muscle strength and lower pain sensitivity in people with osteoarthritis. Participants taking fibre also had higher levels of GLP-1, a hormone tied to appetite and blood sugar.

Read more at nypost.com →

Eating lots of sugar on antibiotics may harm the gut more

The Times of India

A new study reported by The Times of India suggests a sugar rich diet while taking antibiotics may deepen the disruption those drugs cause to helpful gut bacteria. Antibiotics cannot always tell harmful bacteria from the beneficial ones that aid digestion and immunity.

Read more at The Times of India →

Unsweetened cranberry juice may help protect teeth

ScienceDaily

Researchers at the University of Illinois Chicago found pure, unsweetened cranberry juice slowed the regrowth of dental plaque bacteria and reduced the acids that damage tooth enamel. They believe naturally occurring compounds in cranberries are responsible.

Read more at ScienceDaily →

A Chinese study links meat frequency to reaching 100 in underweight elders

streamlinefeed.co.ke

A Chinese study of adults aged 80 and over found that among underweight participants, eating meat almost every day was associated with higher odds of reaching 100, with an odds ratio of 1.44. The report notes this does not prove cause.

Read more at streamlinefeed.co.ke →

A sugary drinks tax alone may not change habits in Indonesia

Bioengineer.org

A qualitative study in BMC Nutrition interviewed people across government, academia and civil society in Indonesia, which has no sugary drinks tax. It concludes that taxation by itself would not be enough, and that efforts across several sectors would be needed to cut consumption.

Read more at Bioengineer.org →
