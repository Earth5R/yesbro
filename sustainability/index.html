ESG and Disclosure

Investors ask the ISSB for a mandatory nature standard

ESG Today

A group of 15 financial institutions, including Federated Hermes, Nordea AM and Rabobank, has written to the ISSB asking for a mandatory, standalone standard on nature-related risks. The ISSB launched a consultation last week on a non-mandatory Practice Statement. Investors asked for a rule and were offered a statement.

Read more at ESG Today →

GHG Protocol delays its stricter Scope 2 rules after companies object

ESG Today

GHG Protocol plans to delay mandatory application of stricter Scope 2 rules that would make companies match clean energy purchases to the time and location of their electricity use. Its consultation found very low support from companies for the change. The companies being measured were consulted, and preferred not.

Read more at ESG Today →

EU sustainable finance platform warns against lighter rules for non-EU firms

wwf.eu

The EU Platform on Sustainable Finance has warned against weaker reporting rules for non-EU companies in EFRAG's draft ESRS 40-a advice. It objects to a 'mixed approach' letting them report only impacts connected to the EU. WWF EU said this risks obscuring major impacts elsewhere in global value chains.

Read more at wwf.eu →

ISSB opens consultation on nature-related disclosure guidance

africasustainabilitymatters.com

The ISSB has proposed a Practice Statement on Nature-related Disclosures, published during the COP17 biodiversity talks. The consultation closes on 19 February 2027 and asks whether the guidance should stay a practice statement or become a formal standard. It could shape reporting by African firms in mining, agriculture and finance.

Read more at africasustainabilitymatters.com →

Morgan Stanley survey finds investors still keen on sustainable funds, in smaller doses

ESG News

More than eight in 10 investors in Morgan Stanley's 2026 Sustainable Signals survey plan to raise sustainable allocations. Only 2% of asset owners expect growth above 10 percentage points, against about one fifth in 2025. Adaptation has overtaken mitigation as the top theme. Enthusiasm remains, in smaller portions.

Read more at ESG News →
More from ESG and Disclosure

Banks' CSRD reports find climate material and value chain workers mostly not

Responsible Investor

A Responsible Investor comment piece reviewed CSRD reports from 74 banks in an EFRAG dataset plus seven large Norwegian banks. Climate and own workforce were rated material in more than 90 percent of reports. Workers in the value chain made it into 23 percent, and Indigenous peoples' rights into none.

Datamaran launches a tool to tell companies which rules apply to them

ESG Today

Datamaran has launched Applicability Assessment, a tool in its Regulatory Monitoring Platform that tells companies which ESG regulations apply to which of their entities and jurisdictions. Many firms still check hundreds of regulations by hand and repeat it whenever rules change. The rules now come with a finder.

Read more at ESG Today →

Lufthansa renames its Green Fare to Impact Fare

ESG Today

Lufthansa Group is renaming its Green Fare to Impact Fare, citing 'new regulatory requirements for environmental communication'. Introduced in 2023, the fare funds extra sustainable aviation fuel covering 20% of a Europe flight's estimated emissions, plus climate projects for the rest. The word green has quietly left the cabin.

Read more at ESG Today →

EQS Group, which calls itself 'a global leader in agentic AI SaaS for compliance', says the Science Based Targets initiative has validated its near-term and net-zero emissions targets. The announcement notes that the issuer is solely responsible for its content. The compliance company has, on this occasion, complied.

Carbon Markets and Climate Finance

EIB Group doubles its climate adaptation finance goal to 30 billion euros

ESG News

The European Investment Bank Group will provide at least 30 billion euros in climate adaptation finance from 2026 to 2030, double the roughly 15 billion euros delivered from 2021 to 2025. Support targets SMEs, farmers and cities, and a free tool called ClimateSET will give clients location-specific climate risk data.

Read more at ESG News →

BRAC Bank to issue Bangladesh's first bank green bond from November 15

today.thefinancialexpress.com.bd

BRAC Bank will issue a Tk 10 billion green bond from November 15, the first by a Bangladeshi bank under the central bank's green bond policy. The three-year zero-coupon bond will finance renewable energy, green buildings and industrial energy efficiency projects as the lender expands funding for sustainable investment.

Read more at today.thefinancialexpress.com.bd →

Safaricom explores a carbon credit marketplace paid through M-Pesa

africasustainabilitymatters.com

Safaricom is considering a digital carbon credit marketplace where Kenyan businesses and communities could sell credits to buyers, with payments through M-Pesa. It would be modelled on the Nairobi Securities Exchange and depends on government regulation and approvals. Safaricom has ruled out trading credits itself for now.

Read more at africasustainabilitymatters.com →

BlackRock's Akaysha refinances four Australian batteries with a $1.23 billion facility

ESG News

Akaysha Energy has secured a 1.23 billion dollar debt facility from 11 banks, moving the debt on its Brendale, Ulinda Park, Orana and Elaine battery projects into one portfolio-level structure. The company says it supports a roughly 6 GW, 25 GWh Australian pipeline. Four loans became one.

Read more at ESG News →

EU carbon market plan would help some ship fuels more than others

indexbox.io

A proposed revision of the EU Emissions Trading System would set aside up to 110 million allowances from 2028 to 2040 to help shipowners switch to alternative fuels. IndexBox says the plan favours bioLNG and e-fuels, while waste-based biodiesel gets no support. Some green fuels are greener on paper.

Nature loss puts 140 million European jobs at risk, study finds

edie

Global Canopy estimates nature loss puts 140 million European jobs at risk, including 20.6 million in the UK worth about 650 billion pounds in annual wages and 21.8 million in Germany. Risks include reduced hours, changed job remits and supply chain problems. The data was published during COP17.

Read more at edie →

COP17 at halftime finds biodiversity goals largely off track

edie

The first week of COP17 in Yerevan found the world largely off track on the Kunming-Montreal biodiversity goals, edie reports. Delegates adopted 14 decisions on education, awareness, monitoring and reporting. Disagreements persist over conservation finance, corporate contributions and community participation. The full roundup is for edie members.

Ministers are considering incentives for carmakers to add vehicle to grid technology as part of a review of the zero emission vehicle mandate, the Guardian reports. Cars would charge cheaply overnight and sell power back at peak demand, helping balance the grid when wind and solar output is low.
