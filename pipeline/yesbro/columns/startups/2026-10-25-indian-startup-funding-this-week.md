Indian startups had a week of contradictions. Founders raised money, early investors cashed out handsomely, a giant IPO was swamped with bids, and listed tech stocks fell almost across the board. All of it makes sense once you look at who is buying what.

## How much was raised, depending on who is counting

Indian tech and startup funding reached $245.9 million across 22 rounds in the week to 23 October, according to Tracxn data cited by [The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/startups/ettech-deals-digest-even-different-lead-as-weekly-funding-hits-246-million-down-27-on-year/articleshow/135152641.cms). That was up 6.2% on the previous week but 26.7% lower than a year ago. [Inc42's tally](https://inc42.com/buzz/from-even-health-to-the-pant-project-indian-startups-raised-over-184-mn-this-week/) for its own week counted 17 startups raising over $184 million, a 22% dip.

Do not panic at the gap. Trackers use different date ranges and rules on which deals count. The useful signal is the direction, and both say a fairly quiet week by last year's standards.

## Where the money went

The top deals tell you what investors want. Even Healthcare raised $50 million in a Series B led by Khosla Ventures, and Different raised $48 million in a seed round led by General Catalyst, per Inc42. A $48 million seed round is not a typo. It is what investors will now pay to back an AI application company early.

Early stage deals made up 70.8% of the capital raised, seed 25.2% and late stage just 4.1%, ET reported. One likely reason late stage private money looks thin is that mature companies are heading to the stock market instead.

## The IPO window is very open

Reliance's Jio Platforms closed its ₹30,213 crore IPO subscribed 22.19 times, with qualified institutional buyers bidding for their portion 70.55 times, [Inc42 reported](https://inc42.com/buzz/jio-platforms-ipo-booked-6-76x-on-day-3-so-far-qib-portion-subscribed-14-18x/) citing BSE data. Retail investors subscribed their share 2.72 times. Institutions clearly wanted in.

Zetwerk, the B2B manufacturing platform, listed on Friday. Its shares closed at ₹138.30 on the BSE, 8.4% below the ₹151 issue price, [The Economic Times reported](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/startups/zetwerks-early-backers-sit-on-up-to-36x-returns-post-listing/articleshow/135149470.cms), yet early backers are sitting on returns of up to 36 times. Four venture firms and an affiliate realised about ₹1,215 crore through the IPO. Inc42 asks whether scale alone can widen its [thin margins](https://inc42.com/features/zetwerk-makes-its-market-debut-can-scale-alone-widen-its-thin-margins/). That is the question every listed startup now faces.

## Public markets want profits, now

New age tech stocks lost over $11 billion in market value this week, and 63 of the 67 stocks Inc42 tracks fell, [it reported](https://inc42.com/buzz/new-age-tech-stocks-tumble-this-week-swiggy-leads-sell-off-with-24-5-fall/). Swiggy dropped 24.5% to ₹176.70 after its results highlighted mounting losses at Instamart. Private filings tell a similar story. Myntra swung to a net loss of ₹581.8 crore in FY26, [Inc42 reported](https://inc42.com/buzz/myntra-slips-into-the-red-in-fy26-reports-%E2%82%B9582-cr-loss/), while Flipkart's super.money tripled revenue but widened its loss by 78.5%.

Retail investors chasing pre IPO shares have learned a lesson too. NSE listed only just above its ₹1,785 issue price, below what many paid in the unlisted market, [The Ken reported](https://the-ken.com/story/retail-investors-piled-into-the-unlisted-markets-favourite-stock-regret-followed/).

## The AI bubble question

Globally, OpenAI is reportedly working on a raise of up to $30 billion at a $1.25 trillion pre money valuation, [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/openai-working-on-new-30b-raise-at-1-25t-valuation-ahead-of-ipo/) citing CNBC. Closer to home, Zoho's Sridhar Vembu said the company will wait for a "less bubblicious time" to invest in AI infrastructure while continuing foundational research, [Moneycontrol reported](https://www.moneycontrol.com/technology/sridhar-vembu-says-zoho-will-wait-for-ai-bubble-to-cool-before-investing-in-infrastructure-article-14059179.html/amp). Two very different bets on the same technology.

## What it means if you are building

Private investors still write big early cheques for AI and healthcare. Public investors want a path to profit. So the winning plan is the boring one, solve a real problem, prove people will pay and keep costs honest. Founders who want to find real problems first can try Earth5R's [FIELDWORK fellowship](https://earth5r.org/fieldwork/), which is built on real fieldwork with real people. The market this week was clear. Growth gets you funded. Margins get you respected.
