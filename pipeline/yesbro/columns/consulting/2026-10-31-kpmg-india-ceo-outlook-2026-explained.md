Every autumn the big firms ask chief executives how they feel, and every autumn the answer is "confident, but". This year's [KPMG 2026 India CEO Outlook](https://kpmg.com/in/en/insights/2026/10/kpmg-2026-india-ceo-outlook.html) is no exception. It is still worth reading closely, because a survey of what bosses fear is also a forecast of what they will pay advisers to fix.

## Who was asked

KPMG surveyed 1,400 CEOs worldwide, including 125 in India, between 11 August and 21 September 2026. All of them run companies with annual revenue above $500 million. So this is the view from large company boardrooms. It says little about the corner shop.

## The mood

In India, 87% of CEOs are confident about their company's growth over the next three years, up from 83% in 2025. Globally the figure is 84%. Confidence in India's own economy is lower at 73%. Indian CEOs are also more cheerful about the world economy than their global peers, at 69% against 65%. And 92% expect to add staff over the next three years.

In other words, they are more confident about their own companies than about the country those companies operate in. That is a very CEO way to be optimistic.

## What keeps them up

Cybersecurity and data security top the list of threats to growth, cited by 38% of Indian CEOs. Global economic uncertainty and geopolitical instability follow at 28% each. Cyber and digital risk resilience leads investment plans for 49%, against 45% globally. Nearly half, 49%, now name agility and faster decisions under pressure as essential leadership skills, up from 31% in 2025.

## The AI bill

AI is a priority for 78% of Indian CEOs. Over the next 12 months, 62% expect to spend 11 to 20% of their budgets on it, and 70% expect returns within one to three years. Confidence is almost total, with 97% saying they can keep pace with AI. Agentic AI is having a significant or transformational impact for 46% in India, against 55% globally.

The brakes are governance problems. Ethical challenges are the top barrier, cited by 64% in India against 57% globally. 53% point to inconsistent regional AI rules, and 70% think the pace of regulation could hold them back. [ETEdge Insights](https://etedge-insights.com/resources/reports-white-papers/87-of-indian-ceos-bullish-on-growth-amid-global-uncertainty-kpmg/) summed it up as a shift from experimentation towards measurable business outcomes.

## Talent and the entry level question

Attracting and retaining talent is the top workforce challenge for 54% of Indian CEOs, followed by skills gaps at 53%. KPMG says 84% are redesigning roles, career paths and team structures, and 77% are hiring people with AI and technology skills. The most telling number is this one. 86% of Indian CEOs are reassessing the skills needed for entry level roles, against 76% globally.

## Sustainability, quietly

66% of Indian CEOs say sustainability is fully embedded in their business, below the global 73%. Yet 68% are confident of meeting net zero ambitions by 2030. The biggest obstacle is decarbonising supply chains. And 43% say they are delaying innovation until regulation is clearer.
