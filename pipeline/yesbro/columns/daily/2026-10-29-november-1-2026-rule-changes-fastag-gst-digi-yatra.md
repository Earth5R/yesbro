The first of the month is when India quietly rewrites the small print of daily life. This Sunday is busier than most. Some of it saves money. Some of it costs money. One part lets you board a flight abroad by looking at a camera. Here is what actually changes on November 1, 2026, and who should care.

## A toll pass for people who live next to the toll

From November 1, private vehicle owners who live within 20 km of a national highway toll plaza and cross it regularly can buy a concessional local FASTag pass, [The Economic Times reported](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/wealth/save/fastag-new-rules-from-november-1-live-within-20-km-of-a-toll-plaza-you-can-get-this-annual-pass-from-next-month/articleshow/135230406.cms). The road transport ministry brought it in through a gazette notification dated October 26 that amends the 2008 highway fee rules.

The price depends on geography. The pass costs Rs 1,500 a year if the plaza sits outside municipal or local town limits and Rs 3,000 if it sits inside them. The government may revise these rates every year on April 1.

The fine print matters. The pass works only at the plaza it is issued for. It is not available on closed toll systems, and it will not be issued if a service road or alternative road exists. The vehicle needs a working FASTag registered for non-commercial use. In other words, this is relief for the commuter whose house happens to face a toll booth, and nobody else.

## GST gets easier on dealer discounts

Businesses get the most consequential change, and it is the least visible. The Finance Ministry has set November 1 to bring in Finance Act, 2026 changes to GST, [BusinessLine reported](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/economy/finance-ministry-sets-nov-1-to-implement-simplified-post-sale-discount-rules-90-provisional-refunds-for-ids/article71631971.ece).

Until now, a post-sale discount could be kept out of the taxable value only if it had been agreed at or before the time of supply and tied to specific invoices. That made turnover rebates and sales target incentives awkward. Now a supplier can issue a credit note for such discounts as long as the buyer reverses the matching input tax credit. Rahul Shekhar of Nangia Global told the paper the change should bring relief to FMCG, pharma, retail and consumer durables, where volume based dealer discounts are common.

Two more changes start the same day. Businesses facing an inverted duty structure, where input tax is higher than output tax, can get 90% of their refund claim sanctioned provisionally. The Rs 1,000 minimum refund threshold for goods exported with payment of tax goes away. Accountants will notice before customers do, which is how good tax reform usually works.

## Digi Yatra goes international, on trial

Digi Yatra's international trials go live at seven airports from November 1, [BusinessLine reported](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/economy/logistics/digi-yatra-international-passenger-trials-to-go-live-at-seven-airports-from-nov-1/article71631668.ece). They are Delhi, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Cochin, Mumbai, Chennai and Amritsar. The trial is for Indian nationals with e-passports flying Air India, which covers all its international destinations, and IndiGo, on select routes. It runs until December 2026.

Setup involves scanning the passport's machine readable zone, reading its chip with a phone's NFC, taking a selfie and verifying the details. The face then works at terminal entry, the security hold area and boarding gates where the hardware exists. Immigration and airline document checks stay exactly as they are. The passport still travels. Digi Yatra says it has more than 26.5 million users and has handled over 110 million journeys.

## Delhi parking doubles

From November 1, Delhi doubles charges at authorised parking sites until February 28, 2027, as part of its winter pollution plan, [Hindustan Times reported](https://www.hindustantimes.com/htcity/htcity-delhi-junction/november-1-jitters-double-the-parking-fee-half-the-car-trips-how-delhi-is-dealing-with-winter-pollution-curbing-measures-101792835003925.html). Parking run by DMRC is exempt, which makes the Metro car park the obvious hedge. One driver quoted by the paper put the new maths simply. Two hours goes from Rs 40 to Rs 80.

## Deadlines that sit next to the date

November is also deadline month. Pensioners must submit their annual life certificate by November 30, at a branch, through Jeevan Praaman with Aadhaar, or via the postal doorstep service, [Mint reported](https://www.livemint.com/money/money-changes-from-november-1-from-pensioner-life-certificate-to-itr-deadline-key-things-that-may-impact-your-pocket-11792832393377.html). Missing it could disrupt pensions from December. Taxpayers who need an audit now have until November 21 to file returns for FY26, after the CBDT extended the October 31 deadline.

## Our read

None of these changes is dramatic on its own. Together they show how India now governs. Tolls, refunds and boarding are being run through tags, credit notes and faces, with the rules written for the machine first. The toll pass and GST changes reward people who read the fine print. Delhi's parking rule rewards people who own a Metro card.

Rules like these get written in Delhi and tested on real roads and at real counters.
