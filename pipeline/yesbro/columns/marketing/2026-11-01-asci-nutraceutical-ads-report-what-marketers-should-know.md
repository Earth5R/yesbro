Supplement advertising in India has a confident tone. Lose 6 kg in 60 days. Thigh fat gone in 12 weeks. Skin seven years younger. The Advertising Standards Council of India has now asked a dull question of all this confidence. Where is the evidence? The answer, in 60 cases out of 60, was that there wasn't enough.

## What happened

ASCI's report, A Pill for Thought: Review of Health and Wellness Claims in Nutraceutical Advertising, looked at 61 ads identified between July and September 2026, [Medindia reported](https://www.medindia.net/news/asci-finds-60-nutraceutical-ads-made-unsupported-claims-225759-1.htm). One case was still under review. All 60 completed cases broke the ASCI Code. In 29, advertisers modified or withdrew the ad without contesting. The other 31 contested and could not back their claims.

ASCI found these ads through a sweep, searching for them itself rather than waiting for complaints, [MediaNama reported](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-asci-flags-60-misleading-nutraceutical-ads/). The 32 brands are mostly direct-to-consumer companies, including Wellbeing Nutrition, Fast&Up, Chicnutrix, Veera Health and Muditam. A Swiggy Raksha Bandhan campaign was flagged too. "Substantiation should precede the promise, not follow a complaint," the report said.

## What the ads promised

The patterns are familiar to anyone who scrolls. Two products, Fitty GLP-1 Daily and Bargad GLP-ONE, carried names resembling GLP-1 prescription drugs used for weight loss and diabetes, and five ads promoted berberine, MediaNama reported. Some products pitched themselves as safer alternatives to prescription therapy. Another 15 ads sold anti-ageing, collagen, glutathione and pigmentation products. ASCI said glutathione ads showing darker skin turning lighter risked "reinforcing the problematic preference for lighter skin." Ten ads were named after lungs, liver or kidneys or sold as detox, some showing smokers' lungs turning from black to pink. One product claimed to "reverse PCOD."

Six advertisers accounted for 24 of the 61 ads. Avangii Labs led with six.

## Why the proof failed

The most useful line in the report is about ingredients. Brands cited research on single ingredients, not on the finished product. "Even where a key ingredient in a product is well researched for a particular benefit, this does not automatically establish that the finished formulation delivers the same benefit," ASCI said. Dose, form, absorption and the people studied all change the result, Medindia explained. A good study about turmeric is not a good study about your gummy.

The other habits were vague superiority words like "faster" and "first" with nothing to compare against, and a tendency to withdraw an ad rather than defend it.

## The enforcement gap

ASCI is a self-regulatory body and cannot fine anyone, MediaNama noted. Its code has legal force only on television, through the Cable Television Networks Rules. Online, it can refer brands to the food regulator FSSAI, whose rules bar food products from claiming to prevent, treat or cure disease, or to the Central Consumer Protection Authority, which can fine manufacturers and endorsers up to Rs 10 lakh and up to Rs 50 lakh for repeat violations. The report does not say whether any of these 60 were referred.

The online point matters. In 2024-25, 94 per cent of the violations ASCI found were in digital media and 68 per cent of flagged digital ads were sponsored social posts, MediaNama reported. The supplement aisle has moved into the feed, and so has the problem.

## What a sensible marketer does now

Our view is that the cheapest fix is the boring one. Test the finished product at the advertised dose before writing the headline, and keep the dossier ready. Drop the countdown claims. Brief influencers on what they cannot say, since endorsers can be penalised too. Treat any copy that mentions a disease as a regulatory document.

There is also a softer lesson. The most persuasive claim is one real customers already make in their own words. Finding it means talking to people, which is the kind of on-the-ground listening and user research that Earth5R's [FIELDWORK fellowship](https://earth5r.org/fieldwork/) trains people to do. It is slower than writing "7 years younger." It also survives a complaint.

ASCI cannot fine a brand. A customer who feels fooled can do something worse. She can stop buying.
