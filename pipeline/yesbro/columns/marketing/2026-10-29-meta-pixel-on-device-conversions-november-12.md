Every marketer wants better numbers. On November 12, many will get them, whether or not anything has actually improved. Meta is changing how its Pixel counts website purchases, the setting is already on, and the timing lands in the middle of festive and holiday planning. Here is what is happening and what to do about it.

## What changes

From November 12, 2026, Meta will measure some website purchases on shoppers' own phones and computers through a new Pixel setting labelled "Measure conversions on customer devices," [PPC Land reported](https://ppc.land/meta-pixel-will-count-more-conversions-from-nov-12-unless-advertisers-opt-out/). It is switched on for every advertiser who takes no action. It applies to datasets that already send events, and advertisers can turn it off in the settings of each dataset in Events Manager.

Meta's own description in the settings panel says that with the feature on, measurement "happens on your customer's device instead of exclusively on Meta servers." PPC Land points out that the phrase "instead of exclusively" adds a second, device side path. It does not replace the server side one.

According to Meta's notice as reported by outlets including the German blog Wortfilter, the feature recovers conversions that never make it back to Meta to be measured. Matching uses information the customer already gave the business, such as an email address, plus temporary device data. Both stay on the device. Meta learns only that a purchase happened and which campaign gets credit. No code change is needed.

MetricMaven, citing Meta help pages, reported that the feature is not active for people in the EU or the UK, needs the Pixel installed directly on the page, and needs a site where people log in with an email or phone number. It also said attribution windows and models do not change. For brands selling in India, that carve out does not apply.

## What Meta has not said

This is where it gets interesting. PPC Land lists the gaps. Meta has not said whether recovered conversions feed bidding and optimisation or only reporting. It has not said whether purchase value travels with the conversion, which decides whether return on ad spend moves or only conversion counts do. It has not said which browsers are supported, or how consent opt outs are handled.

The bidding question matters a lot. Meta's own peak season guidance, as PPC Land reports it, says ad sets need roughly fifty conversions a week to learn, and that ad sets below that ran about 28% more expensive per purchase in its analysis. If recovered conversions count as optimisation events, PPC Land notes, some ad sets could cross that line with no change in actual sales.

Yiqi Wu, chief executive of Aimerce, which sells Pixel tracking to Shopify brands, flagged the change on X and called it "Meta's update in the worst possible moment!" November 12 is 15 days before Black Friday on November 27. Wu's advice was not to opt out by default, but to export Meta attributed purchases next to real orders by day, track the ratio and keep server side tracking running.

## The other Meta date in your diary

Meta has also pushed its revised commercial and self-serve advertising terms to November 20 from October 30, [Storyboard18 reported](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/meta-delays-new-commercial-terms-to-november-20-after-publisher-advertiser-backlash-113364.htm), citing Reuters. Publishers and advertisers objected that the terms could give Meta broad rights to use their content for AI services, and complaints have reached EU and UK antitrust authorities. Meta disputes that reading. The European Publishers Council says the delay does not fix its core concern. Read the terms before November 20, not after.

## Our view

More counted conversions and more sales are different things, as PPC Land puts it. A recovered conversion is a purchase that already happened and is now credited to an ad. Whether the ad caused it is a separate question that the feature does not answer.

So do the boring work now. Record a baseline of Meta reported purchases against backend orders before November 12. Watch the ratio after. If Ads Manager suddenly finds a better week than your bank account did, believe the bank account. Then decide whether to keep the setting, with data and not with vibes. The platform is about to grade its own homework. It is reasonable to keep a copy of the answer key.

The deeper skill here is knowing what customers actually did, which no dashboard fully captures.
