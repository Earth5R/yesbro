Every week, ministries, state agencies and public sector companies put out tenders for everything from metro coaches to coal to school bicycles. For a supplier, a tender is a sales lead with a deadline and a rulebook. For everyone else, it is a useful window into where public money is going. Here is how to read one without falling asleep, using tenders reported this week.

## Where tenders live

Most central government buying runs through two portals. GeM, the Government e-Marketplace, is the online platform where ministries, departments and public sector units buy goods and services. The Central Public Procurement Portal, CPPP, publishes tenders from central ministries, departments and central public sector enterprises. States run their own e-procurement portals, and large public companies often list tenders on their own websites as well. News reports are useful for spotting a tender, but the binding document is always the one on the portal.

## The anatomy of a tender

A tender notice usually tells you four things: what is being bought, who can bid, how bids are judged and when the window closes. Many tenders use two envelopes. The technical bid shows you are qualified and can deliver. The financial bid holds the price, and it is opened only for bidders who clear the technical round. Bidders often also pay an earnest money deposit, or EMD, a security amount that shows the bid is serious and is normally returned to unsuccessful bidders.

Deadlines are precise. Tamil Nadu Newsprint and Papers has invited bids for 150,000 tonnes of imported 4,200 GAR steaming coal, and the tender closes at 3 pm IST on 26 October 2026, with bids opening at 4 pm, the commodity research firm BigMint reported. Shipments are due by December 2026 and January 2027. At 3:01 pm, the opportunity is somebody else's.

## L1 and why the lowest price is not the whole story

In many tenders the contract goes to L1, the lowest qualified bidder. This week Hero Cycles won the Tamil Nadu tender to supply bicycles, helmets and water bottles to 5.32 lakh Class XI students as L1, quoting ₹14,711 including tax for a pair of bicycles, against ₹15,059 from Avon Cycles, the L2 bidder, [The Times of India reported](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/chennai/hero-cycles-bags-free-cycle-tender-bid-115-crore-above-budget/articleshow/135176819.cms). The total bid came to more than ₹392 crore against an allocation of ₹277 crore. Winning the tender and getting the budget to match it are two different events.

## Big tickets, long tails

Some tenders are less about the purchase and more about the decades after it. Delhi Metro Rail Corporation has floated a ₹923.62 crore tender, on behalf of Hyderabad Metro Rail Limited, for 60 standard gauge metro coaches, with international bids invited and comprehensive maintenance of the trainsets for 15 years, [BigInfo reported](https://biginfo.in/news/hyderabad-metro:-dmrc-floats-tender-worth-over-rs-923-crore-for-coaches). A 15 year maintenance clause turns a one time sale into a long relationship, which is often where the real margin sits.

Awards matter too. Rajasthan Metro Rail Corporation has awarded CEM India Projects a ₹921.63 crore contract, including 18% GST, for tunnelling and two underground stations on Jaipur Metro Phase 2, [Metro Rail News reported](https://metrorailnews.in/cem-india-jaipur-metro-phase-2-tunnel-contract/). Award notices tell you who won, and they are also a list of companies that will soon need subcontractors.

## Policy hides in the specifications

Sometimes the eligibility rules are the news. The oil marketing companies' ethanol tender for 2026 to 27 bars cane juice, sugar syrup and B heavy molasses as feedstock, a step aimed at protecting sugar supply, [Goodreturns reported](https://www.goodreturns.in/news/india-ethanol-tender-2026-27-bars-cane-juice-to-protect-sugar-supply-011-1542599.html). One line in a specification can redraw an entire supply chain.

## A short checklist

- Read the original document on the portal, not just the news summary.
- Note the closing time, the bid opening time and any pre bid meeting.
- Check eligibility first: turnover, past experience and certifications.
- Price the whole contract, including maintenance, penalties and payment terms.
- Read the corrigenda. Dates and quantities change more often than people expect.
