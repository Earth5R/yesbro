From Sunday, 1 November 2026, the GST on many delivery charges falls from 18% to 5%. It sounds like a gift to anyone who orders dinner on an app, and partly it is. But the cut comes with conditions, and the conditions are where the accountants live. Here is what happened, who pays and what a business should do before the weekend ends.

## What changed

The GST Council recommended the change at its 57th meeting on 8 October. According to [GKToday](https://www.gktoday.in/gst-council-recommends-5-rate-for-e-commerce-delivery-services/), the Centre issued Notification No. 09/2026 Central Tax (Rate) on 27 October, with effect from 1 November. Delivery services for goods supplied through e-commerce operators will attract 5% GST without input tax credit, down from 18%. Postal and courier services are excluded.

The scope is wider than before. [MediaNama explains](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-nasscom-seeks-delay-in-gst-rules-for-e-commerce-deliveries/) that since 22 September 2025 the rule covered only "local delivery". The new entry covers delivery services other than postal and courier, over any distance, when supplied through a platform.

## Is this a new tax?

No. Social media posts claimed a "new" 5% tax on deliveries ahead of Diwali. The [Economic Times reported](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/wealth/save/has-government-imposed-5-gst-on-e-commerce-delivery-services-ahead-of-diwali-income-tax-dept-says-this/articleshow/134887613.cms) that CBIC called the claim factually incorrect, saying delivery services such as those by food apps were earlier taxed at 18% and are now taxed at 5%. A tax cut being mistaken for a tax hike is a very Indian festive season problem.

## Who pays

Under Section 9(5) of the CGST Act, the government can make a platform pay GST as if it were the supplier. That applies here when the delivery partner is not required to register under Section 22(1). In practice the app pays GST on deliveries by unregistered riders. If the delivery provider is registered, it pays the 5% itself.

## The catch, input tax credit

The lower rate only applies if no input tax credit is claimed on goods and services used to provide the delivery. [CalcGuru works through an example](https://calcguru.in/tax-updates/gst-delivery-services-ecommerce-5-percent-section-9-5-1-november-2026/) of a quick commerce platform charging a Rs 30 fee on 20 lakh orders a month, or Rs 6 crore in fees. At 18%, GST was Rs 1.08 crore a month. At 5% it is Rs 30 lakh. If the platform had been claiming Rs 25 lakh a month in credit on rider apps, mapping, telecom and insulated bags, its net tax falls from Rs 83 lakh to Rs 30 lakh. But that Rs 25 lakh now becomes a cost.

So the customer's tax line shrinks while the platform's cost line grows a little. Whether delivery fees actually fall is a pricing decision. MediaNama said it could not find a statement from any platform on whether fees will change.

## Why Nasscom wants more time

On 28 October Nasscom asked the Finance Ministry to defer the change to 1 January 2027, citing the work needed on ERP configurations, invoicing, tax calculations and credit treatment. CalcGuru noted that as of 29 October no deferral had been notified. The Council also recommended amending Section 9(5) itself, which needs Parliament. MediaNama found no such amendment as of 29 October.

## What businesses should check now

CalcGuru's checklist is practical. Change tax codes on delivery fee lines to 5% for supplies from 1 November. Identify credits linked to delivery and stop claiming them, reversing common credit under rules 42 and 43. Logistics firms should review each e-commerce contract to decide whether a service counts as courier, goods transport agency or delivery, and write down why. Platforms should update GSTR-8 and GSTR-3B mapping for Section 9(5) liability.

Our view is that the rate cut is good policy with rushed timing. Five days between notification and effect is not much for a change that touches every delivery fee on every app. The businesses that cope best will be the ones that know their own delivery chain, rider by rider and contract by contract. That kind of understanding comes from going and looking.
