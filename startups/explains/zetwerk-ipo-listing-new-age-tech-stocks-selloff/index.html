Zetwerk's listing and the $11 billion tech stock slide: what startup founders should actually take from this week

Early investors made up to 38 times their money in Zetwerk's IPO, then the stock fell on debut and listed tech companies lost $11 billion in a week. A plain read of what public markets are rewarding now.

By Yes Bro Desk · 26 October 2026 · 3 min read

Public markets gave Indian startups two messages last week, and they did it on the same Friday. The first was that patient venture money can still make spectacular returns. The second was that public investors are no longer in the mood to pay for a story without margins. Zetwerk's debut carried both.

The exit, in numbers

Zetwerk's ₹5,012 crore IPO combined a fresh issue of ₹2,830 crore with an offer for sale of ₹2,182 crore at ₹151 a share, BusinessLine reported. Kae Capital, Accel, Peak XV Partners and Lightspeed were set to realise about ₹1,215 crore between them.

The multiples are the kind founders quote at dinner. Kae Capital's fund and its affiliate bought at a weighted average of ₹3.94 a share, a gross return of about 38.3 times. Accel India V bought at ₹7.84, about 19.3 times. Peak XV's early fund bought at ₹6.31, close to 23.9 times, while its later growth fund, in at ₹46.30, made about 3.3 times. The lesson is old but well illustrated. Entry price is destiny.

Then came the listing. Zetwerk listed at ₹159 on 23 October, a 5% premium, and fell more than 20% from that price during the session, according to BusinessLine.

What public investors looked at

Inc42's analysis of the debut lays out why. Zetwerk's revenue from operations rose 40.4% to ₹15,913 crore in FY26. Its consolidated net loss widened to ₹1,606 crore from ₹371 crore, including an exceptional charge of ₹836 crore and a ₹453 crore impairment linked to a discontinued civil infrastructure business. Adjusted EBITDA was ₹421 crore, a margin of 2.65%.

Cash is the bigger question. Operating cash outflow widened to ₹682 crore from ₹386 crore, even as the manufacturing order book grew 43.4% to ₹12,370 crore. A bigger order book means the company has to fund more procurement and production before customers pay. Growth, in other words, costs money upfront.

Zetwerk also changed shape to get here. It started in 2018 as a platform connecting manufacturers with vendors and turned into a full stack manufacturer after a string of acquisitions in 2021 and 2022, Inc42 notes. Its manufacturing segment earned a 6.03% EBITDA margin, while the raw materials business, sold under the Terra91 brand, ran at about 1.24%.

The wider slide

Zetwerk was not falling alone. New-age tech stocks lost more than $11 billion in combined market value in the holiday shortened week, Inc42 reported. Of the 67 stocks it tracks, 63 declined. Swiggy fell 24.5% to ₹176.70 after its results showed mounting losses at Instamart, leaving it 54.7% below its ₹390 IPO price. Twelve stocks, including FirstCry, Unicommerce and IndiaMART, hit 52-week lows.

Some of that was the market, not the companies. The Nifty 50 fell 2.63% for the week and the Smallcap 100 fell 7.3%, with crude near $115 and foreign investors selling for a tenth straight week, according to the same report. In a week like that, even good results struggle. Eternal reported a nearly 92% rise in quarterly profit and still went down.

What founders should take from it

The pitch that raised money in 2021 was scale. The question public investors asked last week was what each extra rupee of revenue does to cash. Those are different questions, and the gap between them is where Zetwerk's listing price went.

For a founder still years from an IPO, the practical takeaway is to know your unit economics before someone else calculates them for you. That usually starts far from a spreadsheet, with time on the ground talking to the customers and suppliers who decide your margins. That kind of grounded user and field research is exactly what Earth5R's FIELDWORK fellowship teaches, and it is a better habit than a better pitch deck.

Our view

This was not a verdict against Indian startups. Early backers just turned a few rupees a share into ₹151, and that will keep venture money coming. It was a verdict on price. The public market will buy your growth. It just wants to see the margin first, and it no longer tips for enthusiasm.
